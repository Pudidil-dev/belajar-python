# 镜头表 · Shot list — HOW CT SCANNERS ACTUALLY WORK

*How X-Rays Become 3D Images.* A silent 8-bit pixel-art explainer:
- **Format.** 1080×1920 vertical, 60 fps, exactly 120 s.
- **Sound.** No narration and no subtitles: short context labels, a synthesized minimal-techno score and game-style sound effects.
- **Music grid.** 120 BPM: one beat is 0.5 s and one bar is 2 s, so the film is 240 beats, or 60 bars. Every scene boundary in the brief (0, 4, 10, 17, 24, 32, 39, 49, 57, 65, 73, 80, 88, 96, 106, 112, 118, 120 s) falls on a beat.
- **Loop.** The last bar runs straight back into the first frame: the same skull, in the same pose, on the same monitor.

**The result comes first, then the film works back to how it was made.** One connected journey:
1. A 3D CT skull on a workstation monitor.
2. Out through the screen to the scanner room.
3. Into the gantry: the X-ray tube and the detector that rotate together.
4. Into the tube: the hot cathode, then electrons crossing the vacuum and slowing in the tungsten anode (bremsstrahlung).
5. Out with the beam: filter, bowtie filter, collimator.
6. Through the head: transmission, photoelectric absorption, Compton scattering.
7. Air, soft tissue and bone compared.
8. Into one detector element: scintillator → light → photodiode → signal.
9. Out to the whole gantry: many angles, then the helical path.
10. The measurement p = ln(I₀/I), the sinogram, filtered backprojection, CT numbers.
11. Slices stacked into a volume, the bone segmented, the voxel skull rebuilt: the first frame again.

**Every image in the film is computed from one patient model** (`engine/lib/head.js`): a procedural adult head in millimetres.
- **Anatomy.**
  - Skull: cranial vault with outer and inner tables and diploë, orbits, nasal aperture, zygomatic arches, maxilla, mandible and teeth.
  - Inside the skull: brain (gray and white matter), ventricles and CSF, eyes, paranasal sinuses and nasal cavity.
  - Outside it: scalp, fat and the face's soft tissue.
- **One model, every image.** Each tissue has a linear attenuation coefficient μ at the beam's effective energy (≈ 70 keV), and therefore a CT number in Hounsfield units. From that one model come:
  - the bone-white 3D voxel skull (bone segmented at > 300 HU);
  - every axial CT section;
  - the projections p = ∫μ dl;
  - the sinogram;
  - the filtered-backprojection reconstruction, computed frame by frame from that sinogram;
  - the illustrated head cross-section the photons cross.

**Physics fixed for the whole film:**
- **Scanner.** A third-generation multidetector CT: the X-ray tube and a curved detector arc face each other on one rotating frame. A real rotation takes ≈ 0.3–0.5 s and records ≈ 1,000 views; the film shows it slowed down and says so.
- **Tube.**
  - **Cathode.** A tungsten filament (> 2,000 °C) releases electrons by thermionic emission.
  - **Acceleration.** 120 kV accelerates them across the vacuum to ≤ 120 keV, ≈ 0.59 c on arrival.
  - **Anode.** They hit a rotating tungsten anode. Bremsstrahlung (deceleration in the field of a nucleus) gives a continuous spectrum up to 120 keV; tungsten K lines appear at 59 and 67 keV.
  - **Heat.** About 99% of the electrons' energy becomes heat (efficiency ≈ 1%). The disc spins (≈ 10,000 rpm), spreading that heat round a ring.
  - **Not fission.** No nuclear reaction and no splitting: nothing happens to the nucleus.
- **Beam.**
  - **Filter.** A flat filter (aluminium/copper) removes low-energy photons, which would add dose without reaching the detector.
  - **Bowtie filter.** It is thicker toward the edges, so the beam is weaker where the patient is thinner (less dose there).
  - **Collimator.** The blades limit the beam's thickness along the patient (the z axis).
  - **Shape.** The result is a fan in the scan plane and a thin cone across the detector rows.
- **Interactions at CT energies.**
  - **Photoelectric absorption.** The photon is absorbed by an inner-shell electron, which is ejected. It is strong in bone (calcium) and rises steeply with atomic number.
  - **Compton scattering.** The photon gives part of its energy to an outer electron and changes direction. It dominates in soft tissue.
  - **Ionizing.** Both knock electrons out of atoms: X-rays are ionizing radiation, so CT is used when justified and at the lowest dose that answers the question.
- **Attenuation.** It depends on composition (atomic number), density, path length and photon energy. The values used:

  | Tissue | μ at ≈ 70 keV | Transmitted through 2 cm |
  |---|---|---|
  | water / brain | ≈ 0.19–0.20 /cm | 67% |
  | cortical bone | ≈ 0.50 /cm | 37% |
  | air | ≈ 0 | ≈ 100% |

- **Detector.** An energy-integrating element, not photon counting:
  - a tungsten anti-scatter plate;
  - a ceramic scintillator (gadolinium oxysulfide) that turns X-rays into visible light;
  - a silicon photodiode that turns light into charge;
  - readout electronics that integrate the charge over each view into one number.
- **Measurement.** p = ln(I₀/I) = ∫ μ dl along each ray, where I₀ is the unattenuated reference intensity. The detector measures transmitted intensity only, never density directly. The model is idealized: a real beam is polychromatic and the scanner applies corrections.
- **Sinogram and reconstruction.**
  - **One slice.** Every angle gives one profile, and the profiles stacked by angle form the sinogram: detector position across, angle down.
  - **Filtered backprojection.** Each profile is ramp-filtered and smeared back along its own direction, then all directions are summed.
  - **Few views to many.** With few views the image is streaky; with hundreds it is sharp.
  - **Other methods.** FBP is one method; iterative reconstruction also exists.
- **CT numbers.** HU = 1000 × (μ − μ_water) / μ_water. Air ≈ −1000, water 0, soft tissue +20…+80, bone ≈ +700…+1900.
- **Windows.** A window only changes the display: brain W80/L40, bone W2000/L500.
- **Helical scanning.** The table moves while the frame rotates, so relative to the patient the focal spot follows a helix at the tube's radius. It is not drawn on the skin.
- **3D.** Contiguous axial slices form a volume of voxels; bone is selected by its CT value and rendered as a surface. It is a computer rendering on a screen, not a hologram, and CT is not MRI.

Colour code (educational convention, not real colours):
- **Particles and signals.**
  - Electrons are electric cyan; electrical signals are a brighter cyan.
  - X-ray photons are golden yellow.
  - Electric fields are blue.
- **Events.**
  - Absorbed photons end in a red event.
  - Scattered photons turn purple.
  - Scintillation light is green.
- **Materials.**
  - Tungsten is metallic gray.
  - The 3D skull is bone white.
- **Data and images.**
  - Reconstruction data are blue and white.
  - CT images are black, gray and white.
- **Background.** Deep navy and charcoal.
