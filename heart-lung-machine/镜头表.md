# 镜头表 · Shot list — THE HEART IS STILL

*How a Heart–Lung Machine Works.* A 92-second vertical explainer for Reels / TikTok that works on mute, centred on the machine.
- **Format.** 1080×1920 (9:16), exactly 92.000 s at 30 fps (2,760 frames): 25 scenes, transitions included in each scene's time.
- **Pixels.** Drawn at 270×480 and upscaled exactly 4× with nearest-neighbour; one limited palette; simple flat 2D pixel shapes in the style of the X-ray film: one flat colour per part, a 1-px light edge on the top left, a shadow band on the bottom right, near-black background, no soft glows.
- **Two views.** The surgeon's view from above opens and closes the film (the still heart, the loop frame). Everything about the machine is seen **from the side**, the way a dialysis set-up is explained: the patient on the table at the top (a cutaway window shows the heart), the machine on the floor below, every line with its direction of flow. The view turns with a fold (S04, S25).
- **Text.** Xray style: a small upper-case title top left with an accent bar and one context line under it, typed on and erased; part and particle labels on leader lines; numbered step badges for the workflow. `?notext=1` renders the clean version; `docs/labels.srt` lists every caption and label.
- **Sound.** No narration. 120 BPM in 4/4, 46 bars = 92 s, synthesized in code; a continuous bed of operating-room hum, machine hum and nonpulsatile flow runs under every cut and across the loop seam. No heartbeat, no alarms. Music, effects and mix are separate stems.
- **Loop.** Scene 25 eases into the exact opening frame; the opening caption returns 0.15 s after the restart.

## The questions, in order

1. Why is blood moving while the heart is still? (S01–S04: a machine is moving it.)
2. What does the machine do with it, step by step? (S05: one drop of blood around the loop, seven numbered steps.)
3. How does each step work? (S06 drain by gravity, S07 reservoir, S08 centrifugal pump, S09–S10 heater–cooler and heat exchanger, S11–S12 gas supply and oxygenator.)
4. How does oxygen get into the blood, and what carries it? (S13–S17: one fibre, the membrane, a red cell, hemoglobin, CO₂ out.)
5. How does the blood return, and is it safe? (S18 filter and bubble detector, S19 back to the aorta, S20 oxygen in the tissue.)
6. What else does the machine do? (S21 cardioplegia keeps the heart still, S22 suction saves spilled blood with a roller pump.)
7. Who watches over it? (S23 the perfusionist; S24 the whole loop.)

## Locked scenario and circuit (one reference layout for every scene)

- **Patient.** Adult, anesthetized, anticoagulated, on established cardiopulmonary bypass from frame one. The heart has been arrested with cardioplegia for the chosen operation (an arrested-heart scenario, not a rule for all cardiac surgery). Median sternotomy shown as a stylized cutaway window.
- **Venous side.** A venous cannula in the right atrium → the venous line down by gravity → a hard-shell venous reservoir well below the heart.
- **Machine, in flow order.** Reservoir → centrifugal pump on its magnetic drive (on the cart) → one assembly: heat exchanger (bottom), hollow-fibre oxygenator, arterial filter (top) → the arterial line past a clamp-on bubble detector → up to the patient.
- **Arterial side.** Arterial cannula in the distal ascending aorta, on the body side of the aortic cross-clamp.
- **Other lines.** Gas: oxygen and air → gas blender with flowmeters → the oxygenator's gas inlet, exhaust at its bottom (pale cyan). Water: heater–cooler on the floor ↔ the heat exchanger (blue). Cardioplegia: a bag and a roller pump on the mast → a thin line to the aortic root, below the clamp. Suction: a sucker in the chest → a roller pump on the cart → the reservoir's suction inlet. Gas, water, cardioplegia and blood never share a channel.
- **Oxygenator design.** Gas flows inside the hollow fibres, blood flows around them.
