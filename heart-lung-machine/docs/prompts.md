# Keyframe, motion and SFX prompts (one set per scene)

The film is drawn by code, so these are not what made it. They restate each scene for an image or video model or a sound designer (alternative keyframes, a print storyboard, a re-score), using the brief's constraints as the fixed base.

**Image base:** Original pixel-art medical explainer in simple flat 2D, vertical 9:16, near-black background. Each part is one flat colour with a 1-px light edge on its top left and a darker band on its bottom right; no gradients, no soft glows, no black outlines. An adult on cardiopulmonary bypass during heart surgery, in two views. From above: the arrested heart in the open chest, a venous cannula in the right atrium, an arterial cannula in the ascending aorta above a cross-clamp, surgeons only as teal-gowned arms and light neutral gloves at the edges. From the side: the patient on the table (the head hidden behind the anaesthesia screen, a round cutaway window showing the heart) and the heart–lung machine on the floor below, in flow order — hard-shell reservoir, centrifugal pump on its drive, one oxygenator assembly (heat exchanger, hollow fibres, arterial filter), clamp-on bubble detector — with a gas blender (oxygen and air), a heater–cooler on the floor, cardioplegia and suction roller pumps and a monitor; every line shows its direction of flow. Venous blood dark burgundy, oxygenated blood brighter red, never blue. O₂ cyan paired spheres, CO₂ amber three-atom symbols, hemoglobin lavender, heme gold with an iron centre. No embedded text, no logos, no faces, no gore, no toy-like organs.

**Motion base:** Animate only the stated action. The heart never moves. Blood moves continuously and never pulses. Every line keeps its route and attachment points and shows its direction with moving marks. The centrifugal pump stays a smooth-rotor pump turned by magnets; roller pumps push by squeezing the tube. Gas, water, cardioplegia and blood stay in separate channels. Red cells never cross the oxygenator membrane. One eased camera move per scene; scale changes are visible zooms; the change of view is a fold. No shake, no flicker, no morphing, no new parts.
