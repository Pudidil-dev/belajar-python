# 镜头表 · How Bacterial Colonies Grow on Agar

20 s seamless loop, 1080×1920 vertical, 30 fps, 120 BPM (one bar = 2 s, 10 bars). One continuous camera journey with
no cuts and no transitions. It opens looking straight down at a lab bench, still panning, and stops over a fresh
Petri dish; only then does the cotton swab come in, land and streak the agar. The camera tilts down to ride beside
the tip, dives into the trail down to a single bacterium, watches it split, and keeps pulling back while the cells
keep doubling until they form one cream colony. It pulls out and tilts back up to look straight down on the whole
plate, where colonies pop up along the swab's path. Then it slides off the culture and pans along the bench again,
past the burner, the notebook and the stirrer, to the next fresh dish: that pan is the one the film opened on, so
the last frame runs straight into the first. No title card, subtitles or voiceover; the only text is a tiny
TIME-LAPSE tag and the cell counter (`--clean` leaves both out).

One example runs through the whole film: one bacterium, left on the agar by the swab, becoming one colony.

画风：clean 2D 8-bit pixel art on a 270×480 canvas, upscaled exactly 4× nearest-neighbour: 1-px outlines, flat pixel-cluster shading with Bayer dither, a 2.5D camera that can look from a low angle to straight down; the lab bench and its props, the dish, the agar, the swab, the bacteria and the colonies are drawn at whatever scale the camera reaches, so the pans, the dive and the pull-out are one continuous move
色板：dark teal lab #0E2A2C + pale amber agar #E9BD6E + cream colony #EADCAE + swab white #FFFDF6 + mint accent #6BD59B
角色：无（只有实验台上的器材、棉签、培养皿、细菌和菌落，不出人和手）
