# 审片: film_v1.mp4, "How Electron Microscopes See Atoms"

**Reviewed:** `film_v1.mp4`, checked against `brief.md` and the three reference images. The file is 115.000 s, 1080×1920, 60 fps (6,900 frames), H.264 with AAC 48 kHz stereo.
**Reviewer:** independent. I did not consult the source code.
**Times:** all times are film time in seconds.

## Verdict

**Not ready to ship. Fix the three Must-fix items, then do one polish pass.**

**What is already right:**
- **Technical delivery meets spec.**
  - Video and audio are exactly 115.000 s.
  - Loudness is −14.3 LUFS with a true peak of −2.3 dBTP, and the tempo is 120 BPM.
  - Every strong edge sits on the 4-pixel art grid.
  - The last frame differs from the first by about 1/255 on average, so the loop is seamless.
- **Structure follows the brief.** All 16 scenes land within about a second of their windows.
- **Most of the on-screen physics checks out numerically.** This covers the relativistic speeds, the de Broglie wavelengths, barrier thinning, Z^1.7 contrast and the detector chain.

**What blocks shipping:**
1. **Captions are erased before they can be read (M1).** This is systemic. About ten captions are fully readable for one frame or never, and two of them are caveats the brief requires.
2. **A render glitch floods the frame with gold (M2).** At 20.43 s and again at 23.60–23.85 s the whole frame fills with gold speckle. These are the only frames in the film where mean brightness jumps that far.
3. **The main physics scene overstates scattering about tenfold (M3).** At 75–83 s, about 30 % of electrons go to high angle, while the scene's own caption says "a few".

**Biggest Should-fix item:**
- **The second half barely moves (S4).** From 46 to 108 s the film is mostly locked-off diagrams joined by dissolves. It moves about seven times less than the first half, and that is the main engagement risk.

## Must fix

### M1: Captions are erased before they can be read (systemic), 44.0–107.9 s

**Seen.** Every caption types on letter by letter and then un-types letter by letter. For longer captions, erasing starts before typing finishes. The table shows the worst cases, with timings read frame by frame.

| Time (s) | Caption | How long the full text is on screen |
|---|---|---|
| 44.5–44.95 | 200 keV = 70% OF LIGHT SPEED | ~0.45 s |
| 45.63 | ELECTRONS HAVE WAVELENGTHS (an example label from the brief) | 1 frame (#2738) |
| 50.20 | ~200,000x SHORTER THAN LIGHT (NOT TO SCALE) | 1 frame (#3012), in dim gray |
| 51.07–51.37 | BUT LENS FLAWS A… | never: the scene dissolves away at 51.37 |
| 55.42 | EACH ELECTRON SPIRALS AS IT FOCUSES | never: it peaks at "…AS IT FOCUSE" |
| 55.60–55.85 | NO GLASS – ONLY A… | never |
| 62.40 | ALL RAYS MEET | never: it peaks at "ALL RAYS MEE" |
| 67.75–67.95 | THE PROBE MOVES – THE SAMPLE STAYS | ~0.2 s |
| 70.4–71.0 | HUNDREDS OF ELECTRONS PER POINT | ~0.6 s |
| 106.7–107.5 | ATOMIC-RESOLUTION IMAGE (a required label) | ~0.85 s |
| ~107.4 | ISOLATED GOLD ATOMS ON CARBON | a few frames at most |

**Why it matters.**
- The brief asks that each sentence "remain visible long enough to be understood", and its QC list requires "Context labels are readable".
- With no narration, these captions are the only explanation the viewer gets.
- Four of the lost lines carry points the brief makes compulsory:
  - wavelength alone does not set resolution (51 s);
  - electron lenses are not glass (55.6 s);
  - the wavelength comparison is not to scale (50.2 s);
  - the microscope steers a probe rather than moving a camera (67.8 s).

**Fix.**
- Hold every caption fully typed for at least 1.0 s plus 0.3 s per word. A six-word line then needs about 2.8 s.
- Never start the exit before the entry has finished.
- Type faster (2–3 characters per frame) or reveal word by word.
- Exit with a short fade instead of reverse typing.
- If a scene window cannot fit a caption, cut a decorative line, never a caveat.
- Add a render-time check that every caption reaches its full string and holds it for the minimum time.

### M2: Full-screen gold flash and strobe, 20.43 s and 23.60–23.85 s

**Seen.**
- **At 20.433 s (frame 1226)**, in the powers-of-ten zoom into the sample, a dense layer of yellow gold dots covers the whole frame within one frame. Mean luma jumps from 38 to 115, which is mean relative luminance 0.02 → 0.17. It then fades over about 0.3 s.
- **In the reverse zoom**, the same layer pops in at 23.60 s and out at 23.617 s, then flashes again at 23.80–23.85 s.
- **Uniqueness:** these are the only two frame-to-frame changes in the whole film where mean relative luminance moves by 0.10 or more.
- **Audio:** neither has a matching accent, so they play as glitches rather than designed hits.

**Why it matters.**
- On a phone in a dark room it is jarring and looks like a render error.
- It is also physically wrong. Gold atoms 0.3 nm across cannot appear as bright dots at a field of view of about 100 nm, and they should not appear and then vanish at the next zoom step.
- Two flashes within about 0.25 s stays under the WCAG limit of three per second, but this is exactly the kind of event that limit exists to catch.

**Fix.**
- Bring the gold atoms in only once the field of view is about 10–20 nm or smaller.
- Fade them in over at least 0.3 s, at the density of the final micrograph (about 0.4 atoms/nm²).
- In the 23.0–23.9 s rewind, either leave the gold layer out or cross-fade it.
- Add an automated check that fails any frame whose mean relative luminance changes by more than about 0.05 unless the change is marked as intended.

### M3: The main physics scene overstates high-angle scattering about tenfold, 75–83 s

**Seen.**
- The on-screen counter ends at "HIGH-ANGLE: 12 / 40", and reads 8 / 27 at 81.5 s. That is about 30 % of electrons scattered into the HAADF range.
- At the same time the caption says "A FEW SCATTER TO HIGH ANGLES".

**Why it matters.**
- My estimate: at 200 kV, with the probe parked on a single Au atom and an annular detector covering about 70–200 mrad (the film's own monitor at 2.6 s lists the detector as 70–200 mrad):
  - a screened-Rutherford calculation gives a cross-section of about 0.02–0.03 Å²;
  - a 0.07–0.08 nm probe puts at most about 1.4 Å⁻² of its intensity at its centre;
  - so only about 2–4 % of electrons reach the detector, and fewer once the real probe tails are included.
- The film's 30 % is therefore roughly ten times too high, and it contradicts the film's own caption.
- It appears as a number in the scene the brief calls "the main atomic physics scene". The "angles exaggerated" tag covers the 35–60° drawing, not the count.
- How sure I am: I trust the order of magnitude. The exact percentage depends on the detector range and probe shape, which can shift it by about a factor of two.

**Fix.** Pick one of these:
- Send 40–60 electrons and let 1–2 of them reach high angle, for example "HIGH-ANGLE: 1 / 50".
- Keep the current ratio but label it plainly, for example "HIGH-ANGLE RATE EXAGGERATED ~10×".

Optionally, also show the real angular range: "HAADF ≈ 70–200 mrad ≈ 4–11°".

## Should fix

### S1: Large deflections are drawn as hits on the nucleus, 75.5–81.9 s

**Seen.**
- **The first ("hero") electron:**
  - It comes down about one art pixel to the right of the nucleus.
  - It disappears for three frames while a white starburst appears on the nucleus at 76.000 s. That moment carries the loudest audio hit in the scene.
  - It then reappears leaving at about 45° on the other side.
- **Repetition:** the same starburst fires 25 times between 76.0 and 81.9 s, about once every 0.25 s.

**Why it matters.**
- The direction of the deflection is right, because the electron is pulled toward the positive nucleus.
- But "vanish plus spark" reads as "the electron struck the nucleus and bounced off". The brief rules that out: "Do not show every incoming electron directly colliding with the nucleus", and scattering is an interaction with the atom's electrostatic potential.
- It also undercuts the film's own caption, "ELECTRONS FEEL THE ATOM'S ELECTRIC FIELD".

**Fix.**
- Draw every trajectory as one continuous, smooth, hyperbola-like bend that never touches the nucleus.
- Replace the spark with a gentle pulse in the field rings or in the wavefunction overlay.
- Keep the audio accent, but on the deflection rather than on a contact flash.

### S2: Colour gives away the outcome before the interaction, and the detector geometry does not match the counter, 75–91 s

**Seen.**
- (a) **75–83 s:** electrons that will scatter to high angle are already orange on their way in.
- (b) **83.3–91.3 s:** the incoming probe is orange in the gold panel and cyan in the carbon panel, under the caption "SAME PROBE, TWO ATOMS".
- (c) **Gold panel:**
  - Some orange "high-angle" rays pass straight down between the two detector bars, where only low-angle electrons should go.
  - Others leave the panel above the bars and never reach them.
  - Yet the "HIGH-ANGLE HITS" counter still climbs to 20.

**Why it matters.**
- An electron cannot "know" in advance where it will scatter.
- The two probes are supposed to be identical.
- The picture contradicts the counter the viewer is meant to trust, and the QC list requires that HAADF detection be accurate.

**Fix.**
- Make every incoming electron cyan in both panels.
- Change colour only at the moment of a large deflection.
- Send every orange ray into a detector bar, and count a hit only when it lands.

### S3: Gold atoms are drawn as light sources, 21.3–23.0 s and 68.5–74.9 s

**Seen.**
- Gold atoms appear as glowing orange "suns" with bloom halos, both on the 3 nm carbon film and in the raster-scan view.
- They glow before the probe reaches them, and then the image pixel turns white as the probe passes.

**Why it matters.**
- The brief's Scene 13 says: "Do not show the gold atom emitting its own visible light."
- On a dark background a halo reads as emitted light.
- This sequence invites the wrong idea that the detector is seeing the glow.

**Fix.**
- Draw gold as a flat, saturated disc with no bloom, or as a darker sprite with an outline.
- Save glow effects for detector hits and image pixels.

### S4: The second half becomes locked-off diagram cards, 46–108 s

**Seen.**

| Section | Average share of pixels changing per frame | Share of near-static frames (<1 % change) |
|---|---|---|
| 0–46 s | 10.3 % | 24 % |
| 46–108 s | 1.5 % | 74 % |
| Lens, 53–59 s | — | 99 % |
| Detector chain and ADC, 95–103 s | — | 94 % |

- The camera is locked through this stretch.
- Camera moves are replaced by about ten dither dissolves between schematic panels, at roughly 46.1, 51.4, 52.8, 59.1, 63.0, 66.1, 68.5 and 83.1 s.

**Why it matters.**
- The brief asks for "cinematic continuous movement", "2.5D parallax" and "continuous object-following". It also says "Do not create a slideshow / static educational slides".
- After a kinetic first half, 60 seconds of locked framing is the most likely point where viewers drop off a vertical short.
- The diagrams themselves are good.

**Fix.**
- Keep the diagrams, but give each shot a slow push-in or drift (2–5 % scale change over the shot) with a parallax layer.
- Carry one tracked electron across the cuts (lens → corrector → probe → atom → detector) instead of dissolving.
- Add a brief return to the full column at about 74 s and 99 s, so viewers can see where in the machine they are.

### S5: Labels are drawn over things, leader lines strike through text, and labels crowd the edges (9.5–10.9 s, 37.3–38.0 s, 111.8–113.9 s)

**Seen.**
- **"CONTROL ELECTRONICS" (9.5–10.9 s):**
  - It is a black box placed on top of the microscope column.
  - Its leader line runs off-screen to the right, so its target is never visible.
- **"A NARROW ELECTRON BEAM" (37.3–38.0 s):**
  - It sits on top of an electrode plate.
  - Its leader line runs straight through the words.
  - It runs to within about 30 px of the right edge.
- **Final summary (111.8–113.9 s):**
  - The leader lines run through ELECTRON SOURCE, ELECTRON LENSES, ATOM and DETECTOR.
  - Every right-hand label ends about 36–40 px from the right edge.

**Why it matters.**
- These look like rendering bugs, and a line through a word reads as "crossed out".
- If the film is going to TikTok, Reels or Shorts, the platform buttons cover roughly the right 120 px and the captions cover the bottom 350 px. These labels, and several key bottom captions such as the high-angle counter and the 1 nm scale bars, would be hidden. The brief does not name a platform, so this part depends on where it will be posted.

**Fix.**
- Stop each leader line at the edge of its label box.
- Place labels in empty space, never on top of the object.
- Keep text inside a safe margin. Use at least 60 px at the sides, plus the platform safe zones if it is going to a short-video platform.

### S6: Vacuum scene shows the gun at atmospheric pressure and ion pumps working from 1 atm, 24.0–29.4 s

**Seen.**
- After the specimen holder goes in, the whole column, including the field-emission gun, is drawn full of gas.
- The pressure gauge starts at "1 atm".
- "ION PUMPS" is labelled as doing the whole pump-down to 10⁻⁵ Pa.

**Why it matters.**
- In a real FEG-STEM:
  - the gun stays at ultra-high vacuum (about 10⁻⁸–10⁻⁹ Pa) behind a closed valve;
  - the specimen enters through an airlock that has already been pumped;
  - ion pumps cannot start at atmospheric pressure, because roughing and turbomolecular pumps do that stage.
- The brief asks for a simplified pump-down, but these two errors are easy to avoid.

**Fix.**
- Fill only the specimen region or airlock with gas.
- Show the gun valve closed, with its own gauge already near 10⁻⁸ Pa.
- Label the pumps "ROUGHING + TURBO → ION PUMPS", or simply "VACUUM PUMPS".

### S7: The light-versus-electron comparison is far off scale, and its disclaimer cannot be read, 46.3–51.4 s

**Seen.**
- The green light wave (λ ≈ 500 nm) is drawn only about twice the spacing between the two atoms. The real ratio is about 1,700 to 1.
- The 4 keV electron wave is drawn at about the atom spacing. In reality it is about 1/15 of it.
- The "(NOT TO SCALE)" note is complete for one frame only (see M1).

**Why it matters.**
- The mechanism is shown well: a shorter wavelength gives finer detail.
- But the size of the gap is the whole point of "why electrons?", and viewers come away thinking light's wavelength is only a little larger than an atom.

**Fix.**
- Draw the light wave so large that both atoms fit inside a fraction of one crest, or pull back from the atoms until one full light wavelength is visible.
- Keep a small "NOT TO SCALE" tag on screen throughout.
- Hold "~200,000× SHORTER" for at least 2 s.

### S8: The caveat tags are too faint (34.5–35.6 s, 65.0–66.1 s, 77–83 s, about 97 s)

**Seen.**
- These tags are small gray text:
  - "wave amplitude exaggerated";
  - "ILLUSTRATIVE BEST-CASE VALUE";
  - "angles exaggerated";
  - "other detector types exist".
- Measured contrast:
  - "angles exaggerated": 2.9:1, gray (85, 90, 98) on navy (6, 8, 18);
  - "ILLUSTRATIVE BEST-CASE VALUE": 4.1:1.

**Why it matters.** These tags carry the brief's accuracy caveats, and the brief asks for high-contrast text. Below 4.5:1, small text is hard to read on a phone.

**Fix.**
- Raise contrast to at least 4.5:1, for example #A8B0BC on the current navy.
- Use the same pixel font as the other labels, one art pixel larger.

### S9: Two of the three reference images are unused, so accuracy rules 11 and 16 are left implicit (91–99 s, 106–108 s)

**Seen.**
- **Reference 2 is mirrored well.** It shows a grayscale gold cluster with single atoms, red detection circles, a fitted intensity profile and an atom count.
- **References 1 and 3 are not reflected:**
  - Reference 3 compares HAADF and BF STEM of the same crystal, with inverted contrast.
  - Reference 1 shows a crystal lattice of atomic columns with single dopant atoms.
- **What the film leaves out:**
  - The BF detector appears at 92 s, but the film never shows what the low-angle signal looks like.
  - It never says that in a crystal each bright spot is a column of atoms.

**Why it matters.**
- Brief rule 11 says "different scattering angles carry different information".
- Brief rule 16 says "crystalline specimens show projected atomic columns".
- Both are only implicit in the film, and the commissioner supplied these images specifically as the scientific reference.

**Fix.**
- After the detector reveal, add a 1–1.5 s split inset styled after reference 3: the same atoms bright on dark (HAADF) and dark on bright (BF).
- At the final reveal, add one small caption: "IN CRYSTALS, EACH SPOT IS A COLUMN OF ATOMS".

### S10: The powers-of-ten zoom is used three times in about 18 s, and the rewind is unreadable (19.3–21.3 s, 23.0–23.9 s, 31.7–37.0 s)

**Seen.**
- 16.0–16.6 s: a whip-zoom into the specimen port.
- 19.3–21.3 s: a powers-of-ten zoom into the sample.
- 23.0–23.9 s: a reverse zoom from 2 nm out to the column in about 0.8 s, with the scale label changing every 2–3 frames. This rewind also contains the M2 flashes.
- 31.7–32.9 s and 35.9–37.0 s: another powers-of-ten zoom into the emitter tip and back out.

**Why it matters.**
- The device is strongest the first time and weakens with each quick repeat.
- The rewind is too fast to read.

**Fix.**
- Keep the sample zoom; it is the best scale moment in the film.
- Replace the rewind with a cut and whoosh back to the column, or slow it to at least 1.5 s.
- Shorten the zoom back out of the tip.

## Nits

- **N1. Noise in the opening micrograph re-rolls up to 60 times a second, 0.08–1.0 s.**
  - Seen: the noise changes irregularly. Sometimes it holds for a few frames; between about 0.35 and 0.7 s it changes on many consecutive frames, with mean frame differences up to about 21 out of 255. Real live HAADF data refreshes once per scan frame.
  - Fix: hold the noise for 4–6 frames between changes (10–15 Hz), or freeze it for the first second. Either also compresses better after a platform re-encodes the video.
- **N2. The opening intensity profile is fully drawn for only about 0.55 s, 1.3–2.15 s.** Its axis labels (−0.4 / 0 / 0.4 nm, "FIT") are tiny. Giving it about 0.3 s more would let "nm" register.
- **N3. The monitor UI is blurred into gray mush during the zooms (2.6–4.2 s, 110.6–111.1 s, 114.0–114.7 s).**
  - Seen: the screen is box-filtered as it shrinks, so the scan settings the brief asks for are never legible. The pull-back also nearly stops at 3.0–4.2 s, with only about 2 % of pixels changing per frame, before it resumes at 4.2 s.
  - Fix: either hold the monitor at a readable size for about 0.5 s, or point-sample it so it stays crisp pixel art. Keep the pull-back moving through 3.0–4.2 s.
- **N4. Pressure gauge labels, 24.0–29.4 s.**
  - Seen: the tick mark touches each label, so "10⁵", "10⁰" and "10⁻⁵" read as negative numbers ("-10⁵"). The readout also starts at "1 atm" on a scale marked in Pa.
  - Fix: leave a gap after each tick and write "1×10⁵ Pa".
- **N5. Field arrows versus caption, 38.0–41.0 s.**
  - Seen: the orange field arrows point up while the caption says "E-FIELD PUSHES ELECTRONS DOWN". The physics is right because electrons are negative, but a lay viewer sees a contradiction.
  - Fix: add "(ELECTRONS ARE NEGATIVE)", or draw force arrows on the electron itself.
- **N6. The grid mesh shimmers during the zoom, 18.95–19.8 s.**
  - Seen: the mesh jumps from about 6 to about 30 squares across in 0.1 s, which is aliasing.
  - Fix: pre-filter the mesh, or choose mesh levels that nest inside each other.
- **N7. Recorded data changes during the image build, 102.9–103.6 s.**
  - Seen: as the grid refines, rows that were already scanned are redrawn with different values.
  - Fix: make the fine pixels average to the coarse values already shown.
- **N8. The final atom image, 106.1–107.9 s.**
  - Seen: "ISOLATED GOLD ATOMS ON CARBON" appears while "ATOMS FOUND: 52" also counts the 19-atom raft. The count box overlaps the raft's bottom row, and the 1 nm scale bar runs over an atom at bottom left.
  - Fix: either leave the raft out of "isolated" or write "GOLD ATOMS AND A SMALL CLUSTER". Move the count box and the scale bar clear of atoms.
- **N9. The final zooms and summary, 110.6–114.7 s.**
  - Seen: the zoom out from one atom to the whole microscope takes about 0.5 s, and the zoom back takes about 0.7 s, so the monitor and the machine are on screen for only a few frames each. The summary is text labels pinned to the cutaway, not the chain of small icons the brief describes (source → lenses → atom → scattering → detector → image).
  - Fix: give each zoom 1–1.5 s, and use small icons joined by arrows.
- **N10. The scintillator link is never made, 94.3–97.5 s.**
  - Seen: the scintillator is drawn as a separate block, and nothing tells the viewer it is the ring they have just seen.
  - Fix: add "THE RING IS THE SCINTILLATOR", or morph the ring into the slab.
- **N11. The lab reveal is sparse, 7.5–9.4 s.**
  - Seen: the wide shot shows the desk, monitor, column, pedestal and one pump. No electronics racks or vibration isolation are clearly visible; racks appear only at 24 s and 113 s. Nothing at human scale makes the machine feel "massive".
  - Fix: add the racks and a person or a door to the wide shot.
- **N12. Audio. These come from measurements only; I did not listen.**
  - **Beat entry:** the beat comes in at 3.0 s. From 0 to 3 s there is an impact plus effects over a pad, which matches the brief's cue list but not its "strong rhythmic pulse".
  - **Climax build is mild:**
    - onset density falls from 8.5 per second in the detector scene to 6.0 per second in the image build;
    - level rises only about 3 dB, from 104.5 s.
  - **Low end:** 40–64 % of each scene's energy is below 120 Hz. Check the mix on a phone speaker.
  - **Loop length:** at 120 BPM, 115 s is 57.5 bars, so the loop cannot land on a bar line. The beatless riser at 113–115 s hides this, but someone should listen to confirm the seam feels right.

## What works and should be kept

- **The hook.**
  - **First seconds:**
    - The first frame already shows atoms.
    - The micrograph resembles reference 2: grayscale, isolated atoms plus a close-packed raft, and a 1 nm scale bar.
    - The raft spacing measures 0.26–0.28 nm, against 0.288 nm for gold.
  - **Hook beats:**
    - the cursor appears at 0.3 s;
    - "ONE GOLD ATOM" shows from 0.45 to 2.25 s;
    - a fitted intensity profile follows;
    - the monitor is revealed by 2.5–3 s.
  - **Sound cues:** they hit at 0.00 (sub-bass impact), 0.25, 0.78, about 1.2, about 2.1 and 3.00 s, matching the brief's list.
- **Structure.** All 16 scenes fall within about a second of the brief's windows, and every required label is present.
- **The numbers I checked are correct:**
  - **Electron energy and speed:** the relativistic pairs run from 54 keV / 0.43 c to 200 keV / 0.70 c.
  - **Wavelength:** λ goes from 0.0194 nm at 4 keV to 0.0025 nm at 200 keV.
  - **Field emission:** the barrier is "~1 nm" wide and the tip apex "~100 nm".
  - **Ratios:** "~200,000×" for the wavelength gap and "10 BILLION × FEWER" molecules for the vacuum.
  - **Sample:** a 3 mm grid and about 3 nm of carbon.
  - **Probe:** "~0.07 nm", qualified as "illustrative" and "still limited by diffraction".
  - **Z-contrast:** "Z^1.7: ~80×", with "thickness and settings matter too".
  - **Detector:** the photomultiplier gain of ×3 per stage.
  - **Scan:** "hundreds of electrons per point", which is plausible for a probe current of tens of pA and a dwell time of a few µs. The monitor's settings panel does show those fields, but its values are cut off and too small to read.
- **Mechanisms that are shown well.**
  - **Sample zoom:** the powers-of-ten zoom into the sample, 19.3–21.3 s.
  - **Field emission:** the tunnelling barrier tilting under the field.
  - **Lenses:**
    - coil current changing the focal length;
    - the S-shaped (Larmor) ray path.
  - **Aberration correction:** outer rays focus too soon → multipole corrector → blurred becomes sharp.
  - **Scanning:**
    - double-deflection scan coils;
    - the raster with X/Y counters and a live detector trace.
  - **Detection:** the annular detector with its hole and the BF disk below.
  - **Image formation:**
    - the chain scintillator → light guide → PMT → ADC;
    - "scan position + detector value = one pixel".
  - **Atom finding:** red circles, as in reference 2.
- **Caveats the brief requires.**
  - "THE BEAM CAN MOVE ATOMS" covers rule 24.
  - The "aberration-corrected STEM" qualifier covers rule 19.
- **The loop.**
  - The last frame differs from the first by 1.3/255 on average.
  - The audio rises into the 0.00 impact.
  - The first and last samples are near zero, so there is no click.
- **Craft.**
  - **Pixel art:** the grid holds throughout (100 % of strong edges on the 4-pixel lattice).
  - **Typography:** there is a consistent small pixel-font hierarchy, and I found no misspellings.
  - **Frames:** there are no black or empty frames. The darkest, at 99.3 s with mean luma 14, still has content.
- **Audio levels.**
  - **Loudness:** −14.3 LUFS integrated, LRA 4.2 LU, true peak −2.3 dBTP.
  - **Clean signal:** no clipping and no silence gaps.
  - **Tempo:** 120 BPM.
  - **Sync:**
    - the strongest accents fall on visual events at 3.00, 76.00 and 108.00 s;
    - at 40–44 s the chirps speed up with the accelerating electron.

## Method

**Inputs.** I used the film, `brief.md` and the three reference images, which are phone screenshots of published figures. I did not look at any source code.

**Video checks.**
- Probed the file with `ffprobe`.
- **Contact sheets:**
  - every 0.5 s across the whole film;
  - every 0.1 s scene by scene for all 115 s.
- **Frame by frame** at:
  - 0–0.33 s;
  - 16.3–16.8 s;
  - 19.3–21.3 s;
  - 20.37–20.62 s;
  - 23.0–24.0 s;
  - 37.7–38.3 s;
  - 44.0–46.0 s;
  - 50.13–50.35 s;
  - 51.0–51.47 s;
  - 55.3–56.2 s;
  - 62.1–62.8 s;
  - 70.4–71.2 s;
  - 75.67–76.33 s;
  - 102.5–103.8 s;
  - 114.5–115.0 s.
- **Labels:** read in full-resolution crops.
- **Per-frame statistics for all 6,900 frames:**
  - mean luminance;
  - frame difference;
  - share of pixels changed.
  - I used these to look for flashes, black frames, flicker and changes in activity level.
- **Pixel grid:** checked alignment to the 4-pixel grid on every sixth frame.
- **Loop seam:** compared the first and last frames.
- **Scattering scene:** tracked the hero electron's position at 75.7–76.3 s, and counted the starburst flashes.
- **Scale:** measured atom spacing against the scale bar at 0 s and 107 s.
- **Contrast:** measured WCAG contrast of the caveat text.
- **Caption timing:** no OCR tool was available, so I read caption timings by eye from crops spaced one to two frames apart. They are accurate to about ±1 frame.

**Audio checks.**
- **Loudness:** `ebur128` for integrated loudness, LRA and true peak.
- **Silence:** `silencedetect` at −45, −35, −30 and −25 dB.
- **Level:**
  - RMS for every 0.5 s and for each scene;
  - sample peak and clipping.
- **Rhythm:** spectral-flux onsets, with tempo estimated by autocorrelation.
- **Spectrum:** spectral centroid and band energy shares.
- **Spectrograms** with `showspectrumpic`:
  - the whole film;
  - 0–4 s;
  - 40–44 s.
- **Loop seam:** the sample values at the start and end.

**Physics checks.**
- I recomputed:
  - relativistic wavelengths and speeds;
  - the Z^1.7 ratio;
  - the pressure ratio.
- I made a screened-Rutherford estimate of the fraction of electrons a single gold atom sends into the HAADF detector. Treat it as an order-of-magnitude figure: probe tails and the detector's angular range can move it by about a factor of two.

**What I could not check.**
- **Real-time playback:**
  - how smooth the motion feels;
  - how fast the camera feels;
  - whether the noise boil is visible.
- **Listening:**
  - the quality of the music;
  - whether it is "very addictive" and game-like, as the commissioner asked;
  - the character of the sound effects;
  - the mix balance;
  - whether the film is free of voice. The spectrograms show no speech-like formant patterns, but that is not proof.
- **Phone playback:** how it looks on a phone, including under platform interface overlays.
- **Platform re-encoding:** how it survives being re-encoded by a platform.
- **Judgement calls:** the severity of S4 and N12 is partly a matter of judgement.
