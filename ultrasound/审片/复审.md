# 复审 — film_v3.mp4 (Ultrasound explainer, second pass)

Reviewed file: `build/film_v3.mp4`, 50.000 s, 1080×1920, 60 fps (3000 frames), H.264 at 1.39 Mb/s, AAC 48 kHz stereo.
Reference: the first review of v1 (`审片/审片.md`, items M1–M6, S1–S9, N1–N11).

How I checked it:
- **Frames.** I pulled exact frames every 0.25 s for the whole film and every 0.1 s for all text timing. I also took single frames around every cut and fast move, and contact sheets and full-resolution crops for each scene.
- **Motion and brightness.** I measured mean absolute frame difference and mean luma for all 3000 frames at 270×480, smoothed over 0.25 s. This is the same method as the first review, so the numbers can be compared.
- **Reading time.** I used the client's rule: characters ÷ 17 + 0.3 s, counted from the first character on screen, spaces included.
- **Audio.** I measured EBU R128 loudness and true peak, astats, FFT band energies, a 300 Hz 4th-order high-pass as a phone-speaker model, a 10 ms band-energy envelope for onsets and sync, autocorrelation for tempo, and the sample jump at the loop seam.
- **I did not listen to the audio.** Everything I say about sound comes from these measurements.

## 1. Verdict

**Not ready to ship. It is much closer than v1.**

These problems are fixed properly:
- The gel lesson now holds together. In every "with gel" frame, gel touches both the probe face and the skin. Air appears only in the WITHOUT GEL demo, and sound is never drawn crossing it.
- All six collisions the first review listed are gone.
- "SHADOW" is replaced by "WEAKER BEHIND BONE".
- The pressure wave now shows the medium itself bunching up and spreading out.
- The echo column now contains the fetus, with outgoing and returning pulses told apart by colour and chevrons.
- The numbers on screen are still correct.

Three Must-fix items are still open:
- **M6 (not fixed).** The dark, almost motionless oscilloscope stretch is essentially unchanged. From 35.9 to 41.8 s the mean frame difference is 0.02–0.49 and mean luma is 14–20/255. Including the slow reconstruction sweep, the film stays dark and low-motion from 35.6 to 44.3 s, which is 8.7 s.
- **M3 (partly fixed).** On-screen text is down only about 20 %, to roughly 920 characters, which is about 54 s of reading in a 50 s film. The required line "THE MACHINE CALCULATES DEPTH FROM ECHO TIME." now gets less time than in v1: 2.6 s against 3.65 s needed. It also shares that window with about 95 more characters.
- **M2 (partly fixed).** The end card and title still can't be read in the time given: 3.55 s on screen against about 5.8 s needed (the card alone is 0.6 s short).

The revision introduced no new Must-fix problems. It did introduce three Should-fix items (section 3).

**Speed and timing.**
- 1540 m/s is the standard soft-tissue value. ✓
- 13 µs/cm: the axis shows 65 µs ↔ 5 cm and 130 µs ↔ 10 cm; 2 ÷ 1540 m/s = 12.99 µs per cm of depth. ✓
- 70 µs ↔ 5.4 cm: 1540 × 70 µs ÷ 2 = 5.39 cm. On the graph the bone echo sits at y≈890, which matches 70 µs on the 65/130 µs scale. ✓
- The cursor readouts (24 µs, 47 µs) are consistent with the same scale. ✓
- DEPTH = 1540 m/s × TIME ÷ 2. ✓

**Pulse length and listen window.**
- At 3.5 MHz, λ = 0.44 mm. Two to three cycles last 0.57–0.86 µs, which matches "SEND 1 µs". ✓
- The 156 µs listen window matches the HUD's D 12: 12 cm × 13 µs/cm = 156 µs. ✓
- 96 lines × 156 µs = 15 ms, so the HUD's "FR 30" is achievable. ✓

**Mechanism.**
- Piezo works both ways. On transmit: electrical pulse → thickness ringing → pressure wave (VOLTAGE/THICKNESS inset). On receive: echo arrives → crystal squeezed → cyan voltage blips up the wires → ELECTRICAL SIGNAL. ✓
- "THE SAME CRYSTALS NOW LISTEN" and the SEND/LISTEN bar state that the same elements transmit and receive. The two scenes use different zoom levels (5 active elements on transmit, 7 on receive), so this is stated rather than shown element for element. That is acceptable.

- The cutaway is tagged CUTAWAY. "NOT X-RAYS" covers ionizing radiation.

### 2c. Loop and hook

**Loop: seamless.**
- **Picture.**
  - The last→first mean absolute difference is 0.92/255. A normal frame step at the start is 0.38–0.58.
  - The camera eases in from 49.4 to 50.0 s and lands exactly on the frame-0 framing by frame 2997. The monitor bezel bottom is at y=1700 in frames 2997–2999 and 0–3.
  - Only the scan line jumps, from the right edge to the left, as on a real refresh.
  - The one flaw is tiny: during the final ease-in the top bezel flips between y 323 and 327 (frames 2992–2998), see NN7.
- **Sound.**
  - The largest sample jump across the seam is 0.0075, smaller than the median sample-to-sample step of 0.019, so there is no click.
  - Tempo is 120 BPM in every 5 s window, so 50 s is exactly 100 beats. The loop restarts on a downbeat: the first 100 ms is −9.9 dB RMS against −16.8 dB for the last 100 ms.

**Hook: passes.**
- Frame 0 is a live grey-scale sector on a monitor, with HUD, a scan sweep and a "LIVE" tag.
- "ULTRASOUND" starts typing at 0.20 s, and "SEEING INSIDE THE BODY" is complete at 0.90 s.
- The fetus's arm moves at about 1.2–1.8 s.
- So "this machine sees inside the body" lands by about 1 s.
- Caveats: the fetus is still hard to read (S9), and the machine itself (cart, probe, patient) appears only from 3.75 s.

**Flash safety.**
- The largest frame-to-frame change in mean luma is 46.1/255 at 31.0 s: a one-way cut from the bright yellow fat zoom to the dark crystal row. The next largest are 22.1 at 14.0 s and 11.6 at 35.57 s (v1's maximum was 10.8).
- None of these has an opposite change within 1 s, so there is no flash sequence.

### New Should fix

**NS1 — The title and the right-edge labels moved into platform UI zones (47.15–49.55 s; 26.6–30.75 s)**
- **What I saw:**
  - "HOW ULTRASOUND / MACHINES WORK" sits at y 48–183, inside the top roughly 10 % of the frame that Shorts, Reels and TikTok cover with tabs, search and back controls.
  - During the echo hold, "NO ECHOES IN FLUID" (y≈940), "BIG IMPEDANCE JUMP = STRONG ECHO" (y≈1110–1180) and "WEAKER BEHIND BONE" (y≈1305) end at x 1019–1039, which is 41–61 px from the right edge. That is exactly the height where the like, comment and share buttons sit.
- **Why it matters:** these are the title of the film and the two key reflection lessons. The first review's safe area was y 250–1500 and at least 120 px from the right edge.
- **Fix:**
  - Put the title at y≈250–400, or above the card at about y 1050 if the monitor moves up.
  - In the echo hold, move the column left by about 100 px (it sits at x 436–604 and the left side has room) so all right-hand labels end by x≈960.

**NS2 — The probe's front layer is an unlabelled dark band and changes look between shots (8.0–8.6 s, and every crystal close-up)**
- **What I saw:**
  - In the close-ups there is a 128–260 px band of (49,52,61) between the crystals and the gel. It is barely lighter than the probe's empty interior (35,38,45), a contrast of about 1.2:1, and nothing tells the viewer it is solid probe material: no label, no texture.
  - In the dive (8.05–8.35 s) the same layer is drawn as a thin light-grey lens sitting directly on skin, with no gel under it. The gel shows only at the sides (frame 8.05).
  - Then, from 8.6 s, it becomes the tall dark band over a thick teal gel layer.
- **Why it matters:** the gel lesson now works because air is navy and gel is teal. But a lay viewer still sees a dark gap between the crystals and the body, which is the same visual confusion behind v1's M1. The dive also contradicts the close-up about whether there is gel under the probe face.
- **Fix:**
  - Give the band a probe-material look: the light grey of the dive, or a rubber-lens tint, plus a one-time small "LENS" tag.
  - Keep a thin teal gel film under the lens in the 8.05–8.35 s dive so the two shots agree.

**NS3 — The pressure wave stands still on screen while the whole tissue field streams upward (19.3–21.9 s)**
- **What I saw:**
  - The camera tracks the pulse. The compression rows stay at y≈990 and y≈1136–1164 from 19.6 to 21.2 s.
  - Meanwhile the entire dot field scrolls up by 8 or 12 px every frame, in an uneven 12/8/12/12/8 px pattern, about 650 px/s on a 36 px lattice.
  - The mean frame difference holds at 9.7–10.5 over the whole frame for 2.5 s.
- **Why it matters:**
  - The scene's line is "Sound travels into the body", but on screen the wave never travels; the tissue flows past it. A viewer could read it as the medium moving, not the wave.
  - A regular dot lattice moving a third of its period per frame, with an uneven cadence, is at risk of looking like shimmer or judder on a phone and is hard for platform re-encoders. I could not watch it play, so check this on a device.
- **Fix:**
  - Hold the camera still for the last 1–1.5 s and let the wave run down the screen through a dot field that only bunches and spreads in place.
  - Or keep the tracking shot, add a scrolling depth ruler (cm) at the edge so the travel reads, and use an even 2-art-pixel step per frame.
