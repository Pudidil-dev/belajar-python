# 审片 — film_v1.mp4 (Ultrasound explainer)

Reviewed file: `build/film_v1.mp4` — 50.000 s, 1080×1920, 60 fps (3000 frames), H.264 + AAC 48 kHz stereo.
Method: frames pulled at 4 fps for the whole film, at 10–60 fps around cuts, fast moves and label timings, and at full resolution for every scene. I measured mean luma per frame and frame-to-frame difference, compared the last frame with the first, and timed labels against the client's rule (17 characters per second + 0.3 s). For audio I used EBU R128, astats, silencedetect, a spectrogram, band energies, onset/tempo detection and a phone-speaker high-pass simulation. I could not listen to the audio, so everything about sound comes from measurement.

---

## 1. Verdict

The film follows the client's nine-step story in the right order. Scene boundaries land within about 0.5 s of the brief's timecodes, every required label and both required context lines appear, the opening is a real "result first" hook, and the loop is seamless in both picture and sound. The numbers it shows are mostly correct: 1540 m/s, 13 µs/cm, 99.9 % reflection at an air gap, and plausible reflection percentages.

It is not ready to ship, for six reasons:
- **The gel section contradicts its own lesson.** After "GEL FILLS THE GAPS", a dark air band still separates the probe from the skin, and pulses and echoes are drawn passing straight through that air in three scenes.
- **The film reads like a slide show.** About 1,150 characters of text would take about 68 s to read in a 50 s film, against a brief that asks for "only small labels and very short context text".
- **The payoff summary and title can't be read in time.** They get about 2.5 s together, against about 5.8 s needed.
- **Several labels collide** with each other or with the picture.
- **"SHADOW" is wrong for this subject.** The fetal brain is labelled as shadow, which contradicts the film's own opening and closing image.
- **The middle goes dead.** Over 6 s of a dark, almost motionless oscilloscope graph sits in the middle of a retention-driven short.

The echo scene also swaps the concrete fetus for an abstract bar of coloured stripes, which is exactly the "abstract concept with no concrete object" failure the client warned about.

## 2. Must fix

### M1 — The coupling-gel picture shows an air gap that never closes; sound is drawn crossing air (9.0–18.5 s, 31–35 s)
- **What I saw:**
  - In every crystal close-up there is a uniform dark navy band (about 60 px tall, for example y≈1190–1250 at 10.0 s and y≈1060–1125 at 14.6 s) between the probe face and the skin.
  - At 14.5–16.75 the film labels that region "AIR" and says "WITHOUT IT: AIR GAPS / 99.9 % BOUNCES BACK / ALMOST NOTHING GETS IN".
  - At 17.25 "GEL FILLS THE GAPS" appears, but the only change is that the skin bumps are recoloured teal on a flattened skin line. The dark band between the probe and the gel is still there (frame 17.6 s), and so are dark gaps between the gel mounds.
  - In scene 3 (10.0 s) the transmitted pulse line is drawn inside that dark band, at y≈1245, and continues into tissue. In scene 6 (33.5 s) the returning echo lines cross it too, at y≈1215.
- **Why it matters:** This breaks science rule 4. The film tells the viewer that air reflects 99.9 % (this is correct: Z_air ≈ 0.0004 MRayl against tissue ≈ 1.6 MRayl gives R ≈ 0.9995 and R² ≈ 0.999). It then shows sound crossing that same air, before and after the gel is "applied". A careful viewer learns that gel doesn't matter.
- **Fix:** Draw the gel as one continuous teal layer that touches both the probe face and the skin across the whole width, with no dark band anywhere in the "with gel" state. Keep the dark air band only in the 14.5–17 s "without gel" demo. Then animate the gel flowing in and pushing the air out at 17.25 s. Re-check scenes 3, 4 and 6 so that no pulse or echo line is ever drawn inside air.

### M2 — The final summary and title can't be read in the time given (47.0–49.6 s)
- **What I saw:**
  - The summary card (ELECTRIC PULSE → PIEZOELECTRIC CRYSTALS → SOUND WAVES → ECHOES → SIGNALS → IMAGE, 65 characters) appears empty at 47.0 s, types in line by line, is complete only at about 48.1 s, and fades out from 49.3 s. That gives roughly 1.2 s fully readable and 2.4 s in total.
  - The title "HOW ULTRASOUND MACHINES WORK" (28 characters) types in from 47.6 s, which competes with the card, and is gone at 49.6 s.
- **Why it matters:** At the client's 17 characters per second plus 0.3 s, the card alone needs about 4.1 s and card plus title about 5.8 s. The film gives about 2.5 s. This is the payoff of the film and the frame people screenshot. The card also sits at y≈1490–1875, inside the bottom zone that social-platform UI covers (see S8).
- **Fix:**
  - Don't typewriter the summary. Show it as six icons that snap in on the beat over about 1 s, with one word each.
  - Show the title alone afterwards, or reduce the summary to icons with words of 6 characters or fewer.
  - If the 50 s and the loop must stay, take about 1.5 s from the scene 7 hold (see M6) and give it to scene 8.
  - Move the card above y≈1450.

### M3 — The text load breaks the brief and many labels are on screen for less than reading time

  - The brief asks for "only small scientific labels and very short context text". This film has a header plus a two-line sentence in nine scenes, three 3-to-6-step bullet lists, and many extra callouts: "SENDS SOUND, HEARS ECHOES", "ELECTRICITY IN, VIBRATION OUT", "SLOW MOTION, NOT TO SCALE", "WITHOUT IT: AIR GAPS", "ALMOST NOTHING GETS IN", "SOUND GETS IN", "BIGGER IMPEDANCE DIFFERENCE = STRONGER ECHO", "ECHOES BECOME VOLTAGE BURSTS", "LINE BY LINE, 30 TIMES A SECOND", and others.
  - The table below lists the labels that fail the client's own rule. Times run from the first typed character to fully gone, measured at 10 fps.

  | Time | Text | Characters | On screen | Needed | Short by |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | 5.5–7.5 | TRANSDUCER / SENDS SOUND, HEARS ECHOES | 35 | 2.0 s | 2.4 s | 0.4 s |
  | 9.5–12.2 | ELECTRIC PULSE / CRYSTAL DEFORMS / VIBRATION = SOUND WAVE | 51 | ~2.7 s (fully typed only from ~10.3) | 3.3 s | ~0.6 s |

  - It directly breaks the brief's text rule.

- **Fix:**
  - Cut text by about 60 %. Keep the required labels and the three required context lines, and drop "SENDS SOUND, HEARS ECHOES", "ELECTRICITY IN, VIBRATION OUT", both 3-line chain lists (the pictures already show this), "ALMOST NOTHING GETS IN", "SOUND GETS IN", "ECHOES BECOME VOLTAGE BURSTS", "LINE BY LINE…" and the "REFLECTED" header.
  - Show only one text item at a time.
  - Hold each item for at least characters ÷ 17 + 0.3 s after it is fully typed, or drop the typewriter effect so the clock starts at full text.
  - Pin "SHORT PULSE" to a fixed position instead of moving it with the pulse.

### M4 — Text collides with text and with important picture
- **What I saw:**
  - **24.0–24.75:** as the probe bar scrolls up, the "DIFFERENT TISSUES REFLECT SOUND DIFFERENTLY." box lies on top of the probe, and the probe's corner cuts into the "R" of "REFLECTED" (frame 24.25).
  - **25.0–26.5:** the same caption box covers the left half of the tissue column (frame 26.0). Around 25.75–26.25, "<0.1%" runs into "REFLECTED".
  - **27.0–30.75**, the whole static hold: "0.4%" and "1%" for skin are stacked on each other with their yellow ticks overlapping (frame 28.5). This is 4 s of illegible numbers.
  - **44.25–45.0:** the "IMAGE RECONSTRUCTION / LINE BY LINE…" box sits over the monitor HUD ("OB 3.5MHz" and "GAIN 52" partly hidden, frame 44.6).
  - **2.3–3.4:** the opening "ULTRASOUND / LIVE VIEW INSIDE THE BODY" label fades as a semi-transparent block over bright tissue speckle (frame 2.5).
  - **10.25–11.0:** the transmitted wavefront passes behind the bottom-left chain-label boxes, so the labels hide the very wave they describe.
- **Why it matters:** The review standard is "never colliding with other text or important picture". The 27–31 s overlap makes the first two reflection values unreadable for the scene's longest hold.
- **Fix:**
  - Give the echo column a fixed safe band for the caption, above the probe's highest position, or move the caption to the free right side.
  - Space the skin/fat percentages at least one line apart, or merge them into one tick.
  - Hide the HUD (or the reconstruction label) during the 44–45 s zoom-out.
  - Fade the opening label before the zoom starts at 2.05 s.
  - Keep wavefront paths clear of label boxes.

### M5 — "SHADOW" on the fetal brain is wrong for this subject and contradicts the film's own image (28.0–30.75 s)
- **What I saw:**
  - The column labels the fetal brain behind the first skull layer "SHADOW", right after that skull is shown reflecting 32 %.
  - Yellow echo lines are still drawn inside the brain in the same frames (28.5 s).
  - The A-line at 37–42 s shows echoes after the bone (about 7 cm and about 8.3 cm).
  - The opening and closing monitor image shows structures inside the head ring.
- **Why it matters:**
  - At 32 % reflected, 68 % of the energy carries on. Real acoustic shadowing comes mainly from absorption in bone, not from reflection.
  - A mid-gestation fetal skull is routinely imaged through. Intracranial anatomy is how head measurements are taken, and it is what this film's hero image shows.
  - The label teaches that "bone means everything behind it is black", and the viewer then sees the brain on screen.
- **Fix:**
  - Remove "SHADOW". Or change it to "WEAKER (some sound absorbed by bone)", draw the brain region dimmer rather than empty, and keep the brain's interior visible in the final image.
  - If shadowing is wanted as a point, show it behind a dense fetal bone such as the spine, and attribute it to absorption.

### M6 — Over 6 s of a dark, almost frozen graph and other dead holds break "visually dynamic / optimized for retention" (35.7–42.0 s and others)
- **What I saw:**
  - Measured as mean absolute frame difference, smoothed over 0.25 s, at 270×480:
    - **35.7–42.0 s (6.3 s):** mean luma 16/255. From 35.5 to 38.8 only a thin trace draws downward. From 38.8 to 42.0 nothing moves except typed text (mean frame difference 0.00–0.05).
    - **28.8–30.9 s (2.1 s):** the echo column is static.
    - **16.6–17.9 s (1.3 s):** the gel demo waits.
    - **6.1–7.2 s (1.1 s):** the probe and cutaway are static.
  - In total, about 10.8 s of the 50 s is near-still, plus the 1.5 s blurry hold in S3.
- **Why it matters:** The brief asks for "visually dynamic… highly watchable", and the Kurzgesagt reference implies constant purposeful motion. A dark, mostly empty oscilloscope screen held for over 6 s in the second half of a 50 s short is a likely drop-off point.
- **Fix:**
  - Cut scene 7's text (M3) and use the time for motion:
    - The echo bursts arrive one by one on the beat with the depth marker sliding.
    - The intensity column fills pixel by pixel.
    - The column then flies into the fan (S7).
  - Fill the 28.8–30.9 hold with a second pulse through the column, or cut it.
  - Target no still stretch longer than about 0.75 s.

## 3. Should fix

### S1 — The echo scene is an abstract stack of coloured bars, not the fetus (22.25–31.0 s)
- **What I saw:**
  - The film already has a concrete cutaway at 6.0–7.5 s: a pink fetus in yellow, red, maroon and teal tissue layers.
  - In scene 5 it replaces this with a vertical bar of the same colours, about 330 px wide, which shrinks to about 170 px (x≈435–605) during the 27–31 s hold.
  - Pulses and echoes are 2–4 px lines inside that bar. Direction (down versus up) is hard to tell at phone size.
  - The split at each boundary (part continues, part returns) is never shown as a visible fork.
- **Why it matters:** This is the scene where "sound bounces off the baby" should be most satisfying. As drawn, it is the "abstract concept with no concrete object" failure, and the split-at-boundary mechanism the brief asks for is only implied.
- **Fix:** Send the pulse fronts through the 6.5 s cutaway itself, with the same colours so continuity is kept. At each boundary, split the front into a continuing front that is slightly dimmer and a returning arc whose thickness is proportional to R². Use the percentages as the strength bars beside them. Make returning echoes a different colour and give them a direction chevron.

### S2 — Slide-show template: same header, same framing, same transition
- **What I saw:**
  - **Same header:** nine scenes open with an identical top-left typewriter header plus a grey two-line sentence (x≈48, y≈170–300): ULTRASOUND, PIEZOELECTRIC CRYSTALS, COUPLING GEL, ULTRASOUND WAVES, ECHOES, RETURNING ECHO, ELECTRICAL SIGNAL, TIME OF FLIGHT, IMAGE RECONSTRUCTION.
  - **Same lists:** three bottom-left step lists type in (9.5 s, 31.75 s, 47.0 s).
  - **Same framing:** the crystal-row framing is reused almost frame for frame at 8.6–13.9, 14.0–18.5 and 31.0–35.3, about 14 s in total.
  - **Same transition:** the dither dissolve is used five times (14.0, 21.9, 31.0, 35.3, 42.0).
- **Why it matters:** This is the client's listed failure mode: "slide-show feel… bullet text fading in".
- **Fix:**
  - Vary how text enters. Attach labels to objects with leaders and drop the repeated header-plus-sentence block.
  - Change the camera for each crystal scene. For example, transmit from a low side angle, the gel demo as a macro of the skin surface, and receive from above the crystals.
  - Use match cuts and object-led transitions instead of dissolves. For example, the echo line becomes the wire, and the wire becomes the oscilloscope trace.

### S3 — The opening zoom goes into a soft, non-pixel-art blur and just comes back out (2.05–4.3 s)
- **What I saw:**
  - The camera punches in over about 0.25 s (2.05–2.30), the fastest move in the film (mean frame difference up to 33).
  - It then holds about 1.5 s on a smeared, smoothly upscaled crop of the head (2.3–3.75), which is not crisp pixel art (frames 2.5 and 3.5).
  - It then zooms back out (3.75–4.3).
  - Nothing is revealed during the hold.
- **Why it matters:**
  - The brief says the camera should "move into the image and then reveal how the machine works". Here the move into the image leads nowhere, and its 1.5 s is the softest-looking footage in the film.
  - It breaks the "crisp pixel-art edges" rule.
- **Fix:** Either make the zoom-in a continuous pull straight back to the machine reveal (in through the image, out through the screen, with no hold), or use nearest-neighbour scaling and continue into a pixel-level close-up of the speckle and scan lines that then dissolves into the probe. Cut the hold to 0.5 s at most.

### S4 — An audio accent misses its picture, and the mix is sub-heavy for phone speakers (I could not listen)
- **What I measured:**
  - **Missed accent:** the broadband impact lands at 3.00 s (high band jumps from −43 to −22 dB, low band to −12 dB). The visible reveal motion starts at 3.75 s and peaks at 3.9–4.2 s (mean frame difference 20–29). The zoom-out has no rising high-frequency content (the 2.5–12 kHz band stays at −30 to −35 dB), so there is no detectable "whoosh" on that move.
  - **Sub-heavy mix:** about 63 % of the mix energy is below 120 Hz (20–60 Hz is −6.7 dB of the total, 60–120 Hz is −3.8 dB). Through a 300 Hz, 4th-order high-pass (a rough phone-speaker model), integrated loudness drops from −14.4 to −18.0 LUFS. The "strong rhythmic beat" probably carries less on a phone than on headphones.
- **Fix:**
  - Move the impact to the frame where the monitor snaps back into view (about 3.9–4.0 s), or move the reveal earlier to 3.0 s. Add a short reverse-whoosh on the zoom-out.
  - Give the kick and bass upper harmonics (saturation or a click layer at 1–4 kHz) and check on an actual phone speaker.

### S5 — The transmit/receive mechanism is drawn too small and never shows "rapid sequence" (9–14 s, 31–35 s)
- **What I measured:**
  - **Small deformation:** on transmit, the active crystal's top edge moves 792 → 776 → 804 → 788 → 796 px, a ±16 px damped ring on a 175 px crystal, lasting about 0.3 s. That is about 1 mm on a phone. On receive the squeeze is about 8 px.
  - **Only one firing:** scene 3 fires a single electrical pulse (cyan burst at 9.48 s, audio click at 9.50 s, a good sync). The brief asks for "rhythmic crystal pulse clicks".
  - **No alternation:** the SEND/LISTEN indicator only exists in scene 6, and SEND is never lit. The brief's "same transducer transmits and receives in rapid sequence" is stated, never shown.
- **Fix:**
  - Exaggerate the thickness change about 3× (it is already labelled "NOT TO SCALE").
  - Fire 3–4 pulses on the beat in scene 3.
  - In scene 6, flick SEND on for one frame, then LISTEN on for a long time, repeated two or three times. Add a tiny timeline: "send ≈1 µs · listen ≈150 µs".

### S6 — The pressure wave looks like light beams; the "COMPRESSION" pointer misses (18.5–21.8 s)
- **What I saw:**
  - The wave is three bright yellow and white lines moving over a regular dot grid. The dots never bunch up or spread out.
  - The "COMPRESSION" leader tip sits 25–90 px below the leading front, on empty tissue, throughout 20.0–21.6 s.
  - The PRESSURE trace on the right is good, but it is small and grey.
- **Why it matters:** The brief requires "mechanical pressure waves, not electromagnetic light waves". Glowing lines over a static medium read as light, scans or laser pulses.
- **Fix:** Make the tissue dots visibly bunch at compressions and spread at rarefactions as the front passes, so the medium itself carries the wave. Snap the "COMPRESSION" leader to the bunched band. Tone the lines down to a band of denser dots.

### S7 — The signal → pixel → image handoff is a dissolve, not a mapping (41.9–44.0 s)
- **What I saw:**
  - The A-line and its grey "ECHO INTENSITY" column (40.75–42.0) dither-dissolve into the empty fan.
  - At 42.5–44.0 a separate dotted line with bright dots sits ahead of the sweeping scan line, unlabelled, until the sweep reaches it.
- **Why it matters:** "Signal graph → grayscale image data → ultrasound image" is the key step of scene 7. As shown, the viewer has to infer that the sampled line becomes one image line.
- **Fix:** Rotate and slide the grey intensity column into the fan as one scan line, with the bright 5.4 cm bone pixel landing on the skull. Then sweep the other lines in quickly, on 16th notes.

### S8 — Key text sits where platform UI covers it
- **What I saw:**
  - Summary card at y≈1490–1875.
  - Chain lists at y≈1560–1700.
  - "1540 m/s IN TISSUE / SOUND, NOT RADIATION" at y≈1770–1850.
  - "SCAN LINE n/96" at y≈1880.
  - "SLOW MOTION, NOT TO SCALE" at y≈1885.
  - The "PRESSURE" trace hugs the right edge (x≈1000).
- **Why it matters:** For a 9:16 short, the bottom ~15–20 % (about 290–380 px) and a right-edge strip usually carry captions and buttons. The summary and the "not radiation" point would be partly hidden. The target platform isn't stated in the brief, so I can't confirm which overlays apply.
- **Fix:** Keep essential text between about y 250 and 1500 and at least 120 px from the right edge.

### S9 — The hero image reads as "an ultrasound" more than "a baby"
- **What I saw:** The opening and final image is a hollow skull ring with a few internal dots, a trunk with a stomach bubble, and stick-like limbs. There is no face profile. At phone size (I checked at 270×480) it takes effort to parse as a fetus. The waving arm at 1.2–1.8 s helps.
- **Why it matters:** The brief asks for an "instantly recognizable subject". The sagittal profile (forehead, nose, lips, hand near face) is the image most people recognise at a glance.
- **Fix:** Re-pose the fetus in a sagittal profile, or add a clear profile and hand. Keep the same pose in the 6.5 s cutaway so the two still match.

## 4. Nits

- **N1** The "10" depth-scale label at the right edge (y≈1340) is hidden under the bright image block in every monitor shot.
- **N2** "SLOW MOTION, NOT TO SCALE" is grey on brown at a 3.5:1 contrast ratio. It should be 4.5:1 or better for small text. The main labels measure about 10:1.
- **N3** When the chain lists collapse (12.25–14.0 s and around 35.0 s), three orphan coloured squares with arrows stay on screen. They mean nothing on their own.
- **N4** Static random specks in the dark background above the crystals (for example 9.5 s and 14.6 s) read as dust or particles and represent nothing.
- **N5** "SOUND, NOT RADIATION" would be clearer as "SOUND, NOT X-RAYS" or "NOT IONIZING RADIATION". Ultrasound is itself acoustic radiation, and naming X-ray directly also serves rule 10.
- **N6** "LINE BY LINE, 30 TIMES A SECOND" is on screen while the build visibly takes 4.25 s for 96 lines, about 128× slower than stated. Add "SLOWED DOWN". The claim itself is plausible: at 12 cm depth one line takes 156 µs, so 96 lines take 15 ms, which allows up to about 67 frames per second.
- **N7** "BIGGER IMPEDANCE DIFFERENCE = STRONGER ECHO" sits beside the deep SKULL/FLUID rows, about 400 px below the 32 % bar it explains, with no leader line.
- **N8** The 6.0–7.5 s cutaway, a coloured cartoon inside a cone under the probe, could suggest the probe "sees" a colour picture, close to the camera or X-ray idea (rule 9). A dashed outline and a small "CUTAWAY" tag would fix it.
- **N9** The crystals are drawn as a flat linear row, while the probe face (8.0 s) and the image are curvilinear. A slight arc would keep them consistent.
- **N10** The "99.9 % BOUNCES BACK" leader runs vertically through crystal 5 and its wire (15.0–16.75 s).
- **N11** True peak is −0.9 dBTP. −1 dBTP or lower is safer for platform re-encoding.

## 5. What works

- **Hook.** Frame 0 is already a monitor with a live grey-scale fetal sector, a scan sweep (one pass every 0.5 s), and the fetus moving its arm (1.2–1.8 s). "ULTRASOUND" types in from 0.18 s and "LIVE VIEW INSIDE THE BODY" is complete by 0.93 s, so the "this machine sees inside the body" message lands inside 1 s. There is no title card, room or particle intro.
- **Structure.** The nine steps run in the brief's order: result, zoom, probe, crystals, waves, echoes, receive, time of flight, result. All required labels and the three required context lines appear.
- **Numbers.**
  - 1540 m/s.
  - 65 µs = 5 cm, 130 µs = 10 cm and 70 µs = 5.39 cm, all matching 13 µs per cm.
  - Depth = c × t ÷ 2.
  - 99.9 % reflection at air.
  - The reflected-intensity percentages match textbook impedances: gel/skin 0.4 %, skin/fat ≈1 % (1.7 against 1.38 MRayl gives R² ≈ 1.0 %), fat/muscle ≈1 %, muscle/uterus < 0.1 %, uterus/fluid ≈ 0.2 % (1.63 against 1.51 gives 0.15 %).
  - Skin/skull 32 % implies a bone impedance of about 5.8 MRayl. That is lower than adult cortical bone, which suits less-mineralised fetal bone.
  - "3.5 MHz OB" is right, "2–3 cycles" fits (λ ≈ 0.44 mm), and MI 0.9 / TIS 0.4 are plausible.
- **Mechanism.**
  - The VOLTAGE/THICKNESS inset shows the spike followed by a damped ring, which is clear and correct.
  - "THE SAME CRYSTALS NOW LISTEN" correctly shows echo, then crystal squeezed, then voltage.
  - "NO ECHOES IN FLUID", "WEAK" and "STRONG: BONE" on the A-line, and the echo-intensity-to-brightness column, are all good.
  - The crystals never explode or glow at random.
- **Loop.** The last frame differs from the first by a mean absolute difference of 0.47/255, the same as an ordinary frame step (0.38–0.50). Only the scan line resets, as on a real refresh. The audio continues at the same level with no click, and 50 s at 120 BPM is exactly 100 beats (25 bars).
- **Flash safety.** The largest frame-to-frame change in mean luma is 10.8/255 (at 47.0 s). There are no flashes.
- **Audio measurements.**
  - Tempo is a steady 120 BPM in every 5 s window.
  - Integrated loudness is −14.4 LUFS with a 4.8 LU loudness range.
  - No samples clip.
  - There are no silences of 0.15 s or more below −45 dB.
- **Audio sync.**
  - The transmit click lands within 1 frame of the picture (9.48 visual against 9.50 audio).
  - The receive chirps line up with the voltage blips: 31.67/31.66, 32.20/32.19, 32.88/32.88, 33.42/33.41 and 34.58/34.57 s.
  - Hits land on the transitions at 22.0, 31.0, 35.45 and 47.0 s.
- **Palette.** The screen is grey-scale, signals are cyan, transmitted pulses are warm, hardware is soft white and grey, and tissue colours carry over from the 6.5 s cutaway to the 22–31 s column. There are no blue-purple gradients, neon glows or decorative particles beyond N4.

## 6. What I could not verify

- **Anything that needs listening:**
  - whether the music is "dynamic, addictive", free of vocals and not melody-heavy
  - whether the SFX sound like what the brief asks for (gel contact, hum, bounce clicks, chirps)
  - whether there is a distinct "sharp activation click" at 0.00 s. The track is already at full level at frame 0 and the high band is at −23 dB in the first 20 ms, so a transient may be there but I can't confirm it.
  - whether the ending "resolves". The music simply continues to the loop point.
- **Per-echo accent sync in scene 5.** The echo lines in the column are too thin and dense to time one by one. The spectrogram does show downward sweeps between 23.5 and 29.5 s.
- **Legibility on a real phone.** Label cap height is about 26 px, roughly 1.5 mm on a 6.4 cm-wide phone screen, so it should be legible as small labels. I could not test a device, and which platform UI overlays apply (S8) depends on the target platform.
- **The exact fetal-bone impedance behind "32 %".** It varies with gestational age. I can only say the value is plausible.
- **How lay viewers actually read the hero image and the cutaway.** That needs a quick viewer test.
