# 镜头表 · Shot list — HOW ULTRASOUND MACHINES WORK

A silent 8-bit pixel-art explainer:
- **Format.** 1080×1920 vertical, 60 fps, exactly 50 s.
- **Music grid.** 120 BPM: one beat is 0.5 s and one bar is 2 s, so the film is 100 beats, or 25 bars. Every scene boundary in the brief (0, 3, 7, 14, 22, 31, 39, 47, 50 s) falls on a beat.
- **Loop.** The last bar runs back into the first frame.

**One connected journey, out and back in:**
1. A live ultrasound image of a fetus on the monitor.
2. Into the image and back out: the monitor, the machine, then down the cable to the probe on the belly. A cutaway under the skin shows the fan the probe is imaging.
3. Into the probe: the row of piezoelectric crystals.
4. An electric pulse makes them ring and send a short sound pulse.
5. Gel versus an air gap.
6. The pulse travels into the body as a pressure wave.
7. Echoes at each tissue boundary: strong at bone, faint between soft tissues, none inside fluid.
8. The same crystals listen, and echoes become voltage.
9. Time of flight gives depth, and echo strength gives brightness, one scan line at a time.
10. The fan fills line by line until the fetus appears.
11. Back on the monitor (loop).

**The ultrasound image is synthesized from the same physics the film explains.** The model is a sagittal slice through a pregnant abdomen at about 15 weeks, built from these layers:
- skin;
- fat;
- abdominal muscle;
- uterine wall;
- amniotic fluid;
- the fetus: skull, brain, face, spine, heart, limbs;
- placenta.

Each tissue has an acoustic impedance Z and a backscatter strength. Along each of 96 scan lines from a convex probe (60° sector, 12 cm deep), the echo is computed from four parts:
- a specular reflection at each boundary, (Z₂−Z₁)/(Z₂+Z₁);
- speckle, from deterministic scatterers fixed in each tissue;
- transmission loss;
- the shadow behind bone.

The echo is then log-compressed and scan-converted into the fan. Fluid has no scatterers, so it is black. Bone is bright and shadows what lies behind it. The fetus moves its hand and kicks, and its heart beats at 150 per minute. Every motion is periodic over the 50 s, so the loop closes.

**Physics fixed for the whole film:**
- **Probe:** convex abdominal probe, 3.5 MHz, so λ ≈ 0.44 mm in soft tissue.
- **Speed:** sound travels at 1540 m/s in soft tissue, so the round trip takes 13 µs per cm of depth.
- **Timing:** a pulse lasts 2–3 cycles, under 1 µs. Listening for 12 cm takes about 156 µs per line, so one frame of 96 lines takes about 15 ms: real time.
- **Impedance, Z (MRayl):**

  | Medium | Z |
  |---|---|
  | air | 0.0004 |
  | gel | 1.5 |
  | fat | 1.38 |
  | muscle | 1.70 |
  | amniotic fluid | 1.51 |
  | fetal soft tissue | 1.60 |
  | fetal skull | ~6 |

- **Reflected intensity, R = ((Z₂−Z₁)/(Z₂+Z₁))²:**

  | Boundary | R |
  |---|---|
  | air gap | ≈ 99.9 % |
  | fat → muscle | ≈ 1 % |
  | muscle → fluid | ≈ 0.4 % |
  | fluid → fetal skin | ≈ 0.1 % |
  | soft tissue → skull | ≈ 33 % |

- **Image:** depth comes from echo time, brightness from echo strength (log-compressed). The image is a reconstructed map of echoes, not a photograph.
- **Safety:** sound, not ionizing radiation.
