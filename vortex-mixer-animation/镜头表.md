# 镜头表 · How a Vortex Mixer Works

30 s, 1080×1920 vertical, 30 fps, 120 BPM, 15 bars of 4/4 (one bar = 2 s; the main hits sit on the beat grid). One tube, one mixer, start to finish, on a lab bench. No narration and no subtitles: a short hook title, a small game HUD (RPM and MIX meters) and a few tiny labels next to the parts they name. The camera is one continuous, playful move (close-up, pull back, x-ray, dive into the tube, out again), with game-feel: snappy eased moves, screen shakes on impacts, pop-up words and an item-get moment. A diffusion-vs-vortex battle screen shows why the vortex is fast. The film ends with a tape rewind that lands on the first frame, so it loops.

画风：2D 8-bit RPG-lab pixel art: a 270×480 canvas scaled exactly 4× nearest-neighbour; chunky pixels, stepped 1-px outlines, a limited flat palette with at most a checker or ordered dither, pixel glints on glass. The lab is drawn in three parallax layers (tile wall, shelf, bench). Everything is a 2D side view in millimetres, redrawn at every zoom. No 3D models, no soft gradients.
