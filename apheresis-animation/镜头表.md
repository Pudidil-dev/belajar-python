# 镜头表 · How an Apheresis Machine Takes Platelets and Returns Your Blood

48 s, 1080×1920 vertical, 30 fps, 120 BPM (one bar = 2 s, 24 bars; the last frame flows back into the first). No
narration and no caption box. Short labels are pinned to the thing they explain: a name and, where it helps, one
short line about what it does. `--clean` leaves them out, `--lang id` writes them in Bahasa Indonesia. Close-ups
open and close through a round magnifier lens (about 1 s, eased), never a flash or a block cut.

One example runs through the whole film: one calm donor's blood on its way through one machine. It is drawn from
the arm, pumped, anticoagulated and spun apart. The platelets go to a golden bag, and the rest goes back to the same
arm. The film ends on both outputs: platelets in the bag, red cells and plasma back in the donor.

画风：2D 8-bit retro medical-tech RPG: chunky pixel sprites with 1-px outlines and flat pixel-cluster shading on a 270×480 canvas, upscaled exactly 4× nearest-neighbour; separate artwork for the clinic (donor, chair, machine, tubing, bags), the blood stream inside the tube, the citrate–calcium close-up and the spinning separation channel; soft, friendly, nothing graphic. The tubing is a smooth clear tube with cells flowing inside. The machine is a generic bedside console (no brand): a wide, low off-white cabinet in 3/4 view with a darker diagonal panel, two pump heads on the sloped front deck (blue rotor: anticoagulant, red rotor: blood), black pinch clamps, a drip chamber in a front recess, the centrifuge under a round lid on top, a touchscreen on a post with a red stop button, two IV poles with red and blue hooks.
