# 镜头表 · How an Apheresis Machine Takes Platelets and Returns Your Blood

56 s, 1080×1920 vertical, 30 fps, 120 BPM (one bar = 2 s, 28 bars). One continuous camera journey: no cuts and no
transitions. The camera pans along the tubing and zooms in and out. It goes down into the blood inside the tube,
further down to the molecules in the plasma, back out, and later down into the spinning separation channel under
the centrifuge lid. The finer worlds are nested in the clinic at the place where they really are and resolve as the
camera gets closer. No narration and no caption box: short labels are pinned to the thing they explain (`--clean`
leaves them out, `--lang id` writes them in Bahasa Indonesia). The film does not loop; it ends on the result of the
donation, the sealed platelet bag, shown like a rare item in a game, with effects only (no text).

One example runs through the whole film: one calm donor's blood on its way through one machine.

画风：2D 8-bit retro medical-tech RPG: chunky pixel sprites with 1-px outlines and flat pixel-cluster shading on a 270×480 canvas, upscaled exactly 4× nearest-neighbour; the clinic (donor, chair, machine, tubing, bags), the blood stream inside the tube, the citrate–calcium molecules and the spinning separation channel are drawn at whatever scale the camera reaches; soft, friendly, nothing graphic. The machine is a generic bedside console (no brand): a wide, low off-white cabinet in 3/4 view with a darker diagonal panel, two pump heads on the sloped front deck (blue rotor: anticoagulant, red rotor: blood), black pinch clamps, a drip chamber in a front recess, the centrifuge under a round lid on top, a touchscreen on a post with a red stop button, two IV poles with red and blue hooks.
