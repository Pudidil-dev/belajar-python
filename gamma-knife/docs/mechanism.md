# Mechanism and accuracy checks

What the film shows for each of the brief's scientific requirements (section 07, A–J), and where. The configuration is
one Leksell Gamma Knife Icon throughout. The facts are standard radiation physics and Gamma Knife engineering. Have them
reviewed by a medical physicist before publication.

| | Requirement | How the film meets it | Scenes |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Nuclear decay | Co-60 (27 p, 33 n) undergoes beta-minus decay: a neutron becomes a proton, and an electron (β⁻) and an antineutrino leave. Ni-60* (28 p, 32 n) remains, excited. It then emits two gamma photons in turn (1.17 MeV, then 1.33 MeV). There is no fission, no chain reaction and no explosion. | S14, S15 |
| B | Cobalt-60 sources | 192 sealed sources in 8 motorized sectors of 24 (shown as 3 × 8 per sector), each a welded stainless-steel capsule with the cobalt pellets inside. | S11, S12, S13, S16, S18 |
| C | Collimator mechanism | One fixed tungsten collimator body. Each sector slides along it, which selects 4, 8 or 16 mm channels or a shielded OFF position. There is no rotating turret. | S19–S24 |
| D | Radiation delivery | Emission is spontaneous and continuous. The beam geometry comes from the capsules, the shielding, the collimators and the patient's position. Different sectors can take different sizes for one shot, and not all 192 are always open. | S17, S23, S34 |
| E | Radiation focus | Many paths meet at the isocenter, and their absorbed doses add, shown as a heatmap. The beams do not collide or fuse, and nothing explodes. | S26–S28 |
| F | Normal tissue | The tissue round the target keeps a low, non-zero dose everywhere (blue edges, a dose floor). "One beam" still gives a low dose. | S02, S27, S28, S35 |
| G | Compton scattering | The photon gives part of its energy to an electron and leaves with a longer wavelength (lower energy) in a new direction. The recoil electron then ionizes molecules along its track. | S03, S37, S38 |
| H | DNA damage | Water radiolysis gives reactive species: •OH and H•, and H⁺ from ionization. Direct hits and radicals (the indirect effect) break the strands, and a double-strand break is shown locally. The damaged cell fails to divide, which is a response over time. Nothing explodes and the tumour does not vanish. | S04, S05, S39–S42 |
| I | Patient positioning | A custom thermoplastic mask (frameless workflow) and the Icon's integrated CBCT. MRI defines the lesion and the plan sets the target, beams and dose. | S07–S10 |
| J | Motion management | An infrared camera tracks a reflective marker on the nose. When the motion passes the tolerance the delivery is interrupted: every sector moves to its shielded OFF position, while the sources keep decaying. | S43–S45 |

## Corrections to the storyboard

Where the storyboard and the brief's "no fake physics" rule disagreed, the film keeps the composition and follows the
physics:

- **Water ionization (panels 4 and 38).** The storyboard labels a product OH⁻ (hydroxide). Ionizing water gives
  H₂O⁺, which becomes H⁺ and the **•OH radical**, so the film labels it OH•, as panels 39–40 already do.
- **Beta decay (panel 14).** The brief asks for the antineutrino, so it is shown (a small grey particle, labelled).

## Simplifications

- **Visible radiation.** The beams and photon paths are conceptual overlays. Gamma radiation is invisible and inaudible,
  and the "energy" sounds are illustrative.
- **Beam count.** 192 beams converge in reality. The head shows 44 paths so they stay readable.
- **Scales.** Machine, tissue, cell, molecule and nucleus are shown at magnifications orders of magnitude apart, and
  every change of scale is a visible zoom or dissolve.
- **Colours.** The source pellets are drawn gold, as in the client's storyboard; the brief's colour sheet suggested
  cyan-blue. Electrons are violet spheres (storyboard) with cyan and yellow tracks. Molecule colours are conventions.
- **Interior.** The cutaway, the sector ring and the collimator block are schematic. The sector-ring view is a
  conceptual front view of the 8 sectors round the head, not an engineering drawing.
