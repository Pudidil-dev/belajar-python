# 镜头表 · Shot list — WHY THIS BALLOON?

*How an Autorefractor Measures Your Eyes.* A 63-second vertical explainer that works on mute.
- **Format.** 1080×1920 (9:16), exactly 63 s: 21 connected scenes of exactly 3 s. Delivered at 30 fps; the engine is time-based, so 60 fps is one flag.
- **Pixels.** Drawn at 360×640 and upscaled exactly 3× with nearest-neighbour. One limited palette, 4×4 ordered dithering. Anatomy, optical surfaces and the instrument stay smooth; the pixel grid, the dither and stepped details carry the 16-bit look.
- **Text.** Short English captions, one dominant caption at a time, drawn by the editor layer (`CAP`). `?notext=1` renders the clean version; `docs/labels.srt` lists every label for re-editing. The instrument's own result (SPH / CYL / AXIS) is picture content and stays in both versions.
- **Sound.** No narration. A 120 BPM score and picture-synced effects synthesized in code, delivered as stems so a voice-over can be added.
- **Music grid.** One scene is 6 beats; the film is 126 beats. The harmony changes once per scene: D – A – Bm – G five times (scenes 1–20), then A in scene 21, which resolves into D when the video restarts.
- **Loop.** Scene 21 eases into the exact opening composition: same balloon, road, vignette, focus and lighting; the caption returns only after the restart.

## The two questions

1. **Why do I look at this balloon?** Answered by 12 s: it holds the gaze steady (scene 3) and lets the instrument relax the eye's focusing (scene 4).
2. **How does the machine turn light into numbers?** Scenes 5–18: invisible infrared goes in, a little comes back from the back of the eye, a sensor turns the returning pattern into charge and then data, and the pattern's size and shape give an estimated refraction.

## Assets locked for the whole film

- **A. The balloon landscape** (`engine/lib/balloon.js`). An original picture:
  - a coral-and-cream hot-air balloon with a zigzag band;
  - a narrow winding road and muted green fields;
  - two ranges of distant blue hills under a pale sky, and four flat clouds.

  It is a picture inside the instrument (a lit slide), never a physical balloon. It is drawn procedurally, so the full-frame view, the round patient-view inset and the tiny slide in the cutaway are the same picture.
- **B. The patient.** One calm adult, seen only from behind and to the side in the exterior view (hair, ear, cheek, shoulder), and as a muted profile silhouette round the eye in the cutaways. No face, no expression. Chin on the chinrest, forehead on the forehead band, right eye on the window's axis.
- **C. The autorefractor** (`engine/lib/exterior.js`). An original unbranded tabletop instrument: ivory measuring head and column, graphite base and stage, a teal ring round the viewing window, joystick, chinrest posts with chin cup and forehead band.
- **D. The eye** (`engine/lib/eye.js`). One model in millimetres:
  - cornea: front radius 7.8 mm, 0.55 mm thick;
  - anterior chamber, iris with a 4 mm pupil, ciliary body and zonules;
  - a biconvex lens behind the iris: radii 10.2 and 6.0 mm, 4 mm thick;
  - clear vitreous; retina, choroid and sclera; axial length 24 mm.

  Rays are traced through these surfaces with Snell's law. The lens index is tuned once so that a distant point focuses on the retina.
- **E. The optical diagram** (`engine/lib/optics.js`). One side cutaway, patient's eye on the left, three arms:
  - **Fixation arm (up, amber):** lamp → balloon slide → movable fogging lens → dichroic mirror → objective → eye.
  - **Source arm (straight back, cyan):** infrared source → projection lens → beam splitter → dichroic → objective → centre of the pupil → retina.
  - **Receiving arm (down, magenta):** from the retina out through a ring zone of the pupil → objective → dichroic → beam splitter → relay lens → ring lens → image sensor.

  It is a simplified educational layout, not an engineering reconstruction.
- **F. The sensor and its pattern** (`engine/lib/sensor.js`). A monochrome image sensor (64 × 64 schematic pixels) and the ring image on it. The ring is slightly elliptical with horizontal and vertical principal axes, and is compared with a dashed calibration circle.
- **G. The result.** One fictional example, identical on the screen and on the printout: **SPH −2.00 D · CYL −0.75 D · AXIS 180°**, tagged "Example".

## Science fixed for the whole film

- **Architecture.** A retinal-return, ring-image autorefractor (reference: NIDEK AR-1 / ARK-1 family documentation). No rotating prism, no lenslet grid, no air puff, no fundus photo, no OCT, no topography.
- **Fixation.** The balloon is a fixation picture; looking at it keeps the eye steady and on the instrument's axis.
- **Fogging.** The instrument moves the picture's optical distance a little beyond the eye's far point, so it looks slightly soft and the eye tends to relax its focusing. It reduces accommodation; it does not guarantee that it disappears.
- **Measurement light.** Near-infrared, invisible to the patient.
  - **The colours are overlays.** Cyan (outgoing) and magenta (returning) mark direction of travel only; reflection does not change the light's colour. The patient sees none of it.
  - **Out.** The light goes in as a thin beam through the centre of the pupil and is bent by the cornea and the lens.
  - **Back.** At the back of the eye it scatters diffusely. Most is absorbed; a small part comes back out through the pupil. It is not a mirror reflection.
- **Sensor.** Light absorbed in the silicon of a pixel frees electron–hole pairs. The electrons collect in the pixel's well as a charge packet (pale gold), which is read out, amplified and digitized into an intensity map.
- **Refraction.**
  - **Focus.** A distant point focuses on the retina, or in front of it in a myopic eye.
  - **Astigmatism.** Two perpendicular meridians have different powers and two focal positions.
  - **Why a pattern.** The ring's size and shape change with the eye's refraction, so comparing it with the calibration gives sphere, cylinder and axis. No diopter value is assigned to a ring size or angle on screen.
- **Result.** Several readings are taken and compared. The printed numbers are an estimate that starts a clinical refraction, not a finished prescription. The examination changes nothing in the eye.
- **Honesty.** Slow light traces are visualizations. The microscopic scenes are schematic and not to scale. The comparison eyes of scenes 13–14 are separate models, not the patient.
