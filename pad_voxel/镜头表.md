# How Diabetes Damages Leg Arteries — from walking pain to CT angiography — shot list (镜头表)

Project: 9:16, smooth 3D pixel art drawn on a 360×640 grid shown exactly 3× nearest-neighbour (1080×1920), 60 fps,
exactly 63.000 s = 3,780 frames, 21 scenes of exactly 3 s (one bar each at 80 BPM; every event sits on the beat or
the eighth). Five acts as the brief's music map: symptom 0–6, chronic disease in the wall 6–24, flow limitation and
walking pain 24–39, CT angiography 39–57, comparison and diagnosis 57–63. Not a loop: it ends on the diagnosis.
Rendering (v2, the look of the earlier CTA film): the same small software 3D pixel renderer (Node, no browser) —
smooth meshes (signed-distance iso-surfaces and swept tubes) lit per vertex in limited palette ramps with ordered-dither
steps, rim light, small specular highlights and 1-px object outlines; skin, body and vessel walls are glass
(fresnel screen-door dither), never blur. The artery close-ups (scenes 4–11) mix 3D and 2D: a 3D half-tube lumen with
glossy biconcave red cells, and a 2D pixel-art cut face drawn pixel by pixel (intima, media, adventitia, plaque with
lipid core and cap, white calcium, the organised occlusion). v1 (cube voxels on 540×960 ×2) was replaced because cells,
plaque and thin vessels broke up into blocks and noise; see docs/REVISION_ANALYSIS.md.
Motion (v3, the client's second note: "less robotic, smoother, more natural"): the walk is planned from footfalls —
each heel strike has a time and a place, the planted foot rolls heel → flat → toes without sliding, the swinging foot
travels on a smooth arc, the hips ride the legs' reach (highest over the standing foot, lowest with both feet down)
and sway, turn and dip; knees and hips follow by IK, the shoulders turn against the hips, the arms swing a moment
late with the elbows bending. The stop and the reach for the calf are overlapping actions (knees, back, then the arm
on an arc), with heavy breathing; cameras glide through their keys without stopping; disease grows continuously;
cells and glucose ride the same pulsing flow. The CT act follows a real leg-artery CT angiogram (v3): the patient lies
head first with raised arms on the table and glides into the gantry under the positioning laser; the console shows
the topogram, the scan line and the axial images (computed from the same anatomy and lesion map); the slices stack
into a volume and become the 3D reconstruction.
2D + 3D (v4, the final revision — "combine 2D and 3D so the result is clearer"): a 2D pixel-art explanatory layer sits
over the 3D scenes, without text: a cyan reticle marks where the camera dives into the thigh artery; a small picture
of his leg (the locator) shows where each close-up is; the artery's 2D cross-sections open beside its 3D lengthwise
cut (open ring / narrowed ring / filled ring), and return as small icons beside the findings in the final image;
arrows ride the collaterals round the block; a strip of icons follows the contrast's route (arm vein → right heart →
lungs → left heart → legs); orange rings mark the two lesions on the console's CT images; a second axial inset shows
the block on CTA; pulses of flow travel down both trees of the final comparison. The walking scenes get an evening
park in 2D parallax layers (moon, stars, skyline, trees, lawn) behind the 3D figure, who now has hands with fingers,
hems, ribbed socks and laces.
One patient, one symptomatic leg (the RIGHT leg), one fixed lesion map used by every view:
L1 a flow-limiting stenosis of the right superficial femoral artery (mid thigh, eccentric plaque with calcium);
L2 a separate chronic occlusion of the right popliteal artery above the knee joint, with geniculate collaterals
from the distal femoral artery that refill the below-knee popliteal; L3 multifocal below-knee disease — two
stenoses of the anterior tibial artery, a narrowed peroneal artery, medial (rail-track) calcification of the tibial
arteries; the posterior tibial artery stays open to the foot. The left leg has milder disease (calcified tibial
arteries, one mild stenosis); the normal tree is a separate teaching model.
Text: the brief's English placeholder labels only, all from one editable table (src/04_labels_en.js); a clean version
renders without any text. No narration (Azerbaijani voice-over is added later): the mix is a quiet bed.
Grammar: y1 Kurzgesagt structure — every idea a concrete object, scale travel by pushing through an object into the
next world (skin → muscle → artery → lumen), the subject settles while the environment keeps a slow pulse.

Notes kept with the table:
- **Medicine:** chronic symptomatic PAD only — no embolus, acute ischaemia, ulcer, gangrene, amputation, balloon,
  stent or bypass; glucose never forms a plug; plaque stays inside the wall; calcium is opaque white and never
  confused with contrast; the stenosis keeps a continuous channel (faster jet at the throat); the occlusion has no
  channel and keeps its wall outline; collaterals bypass, they do not reopen; rest lowers demand, the lesions stay;
  CTA shows anatomy and does not treat; the white-blue contrast is a teaching overlay; no numbers anywhere.
- **Continuity:** the right leg is the symptomatic leg in every shot; L1, L2 and L3 sit at the same places in the
  leg cutaway, the vessel close-ups, the filling animation, the CTA views and the final comparison; anatomy is
  never mirrored.
- **Text:** every label comes from src/04_labels_en.js and can be replaced (Azerbaijani letters are in the pixel font);
  the clean version has no text at all.
