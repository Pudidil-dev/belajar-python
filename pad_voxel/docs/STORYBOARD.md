# Storyboard and scene prompts — How Diabetes Damages Leg Arteries

63.000 s · 9:16 · 1080×1920 (a 360×640 pixel grid shown exactly 3× nearest-neighbour) · 60 fps · 21 scenes × 3 s.
The picture sheet is `docs/storyboard.png` (one labeled frame per scene); the shot list with camera moves is `镜头表.md`.
Version 2 (the look of the earlier CTA film); what changed from version 1 and why: `docs/REVISION_ANALYSIS.md`.

**Shared style for every prompt below.** 3D pixel-art medical animation in the look of the CTA film: smooth shaded
models (no cubes) lit in limited five-step material ramps with ordered-dither steps between the tones, a soft rim light,
small specular highlights and a 1-px dark outline around every object; no anti-aliasing, no blur, no bloom, no glow.
Skin, the body and vessel walls become glass with a dithered screen door, densest at the silhouette. The artery
close-ups mix 3D and 2D: a 3D half-tube (the back half of the lumen) with glossy biconcave red cells, and a flat
pixel-art cut face where the wall's layers and the disease inside the wall are drawn pixel by pixel. Deep navy sky
`#101827` → `#060A12`. Colour code (fixed for the whole film): arteries and the lining red-pink `#D45A59`, blood cells
dark red, veins steel blue, yellow-orange plaque `#F0A93B` with a cream fibrous cap, opaque white calcium `#F2F4F6`
with a dark outline, an orange-brown occlusion plug, red-brown muscle that turns orange `#FF8A3D` where it hurts,
white-blue contrast `#CDEBFF` (a teaching overlay — contrast does not glow), warm ivory-grey `#BEBCB0`-to-`#F4F1E6` for the CTA
volume rendering, grey for the axial CT slice. One patient, framed from the neck down; his face is never shown.
The symptomatic leg is always the RIGHT leg. No numbers anywhere.

### S01 · 0:00–0:03 · WALKING
**Camera:** lateral tracking from the patient's right at hip height (32° lens), lead room ahead of him.
**Prompt:** A middle-aged man in a grey-blue T-shirt, dark knee-length shorts, white socks and grey trainers walks across a floor of large dark-navy checker tiles, framed from the shoulders down; smooth shaded skin with a rim light, natural adult proportions, arms swinging opposite the legs, heel strikes on each beat; soft contact shadows under the feet; the tiles slide past to show his speed. Text: *Pain while walking?* (optional).

### S02 · 0:03–0:06 · THE PATIENT STOPS
**Camera:** slow push in toward the right calf.
**Prompt:** His stride shortens over three steps; he stops, bends forward and holds the back of his right calf with his right hand; the calf skin warms to orange from inside (pain on exertion) and short orange pixel strokes flick out from it on the beat. His head stays out of frame.

### S03 · 0:06–0:09 · ENTER THE LEG
**Camera:** fast push: behind the knee, then up along the artery, then a dive into its lumen.
**Prompt:** The right leg turns to dithered glass; inside it the red-brown calf muscles (gastrocnemius, soleus) and the red arteries (popliteal, anterior tibial, tibioperoneal trunk, posterior tibial, peroneal) with faint bones; the muscle fades in turn. The camera rises along the superficial femoral artery in the thigh and dives into it at the site of the future stenosis; a soft dithered crossfade into scene 4.

### S04 · 0:09–0:12 · NORMAL REFERENCE
**Camera:** hold (a short settle after the dive).
**Prompt:** A healthy artery cut lengthwise, standing vertical, blood flowing down the screen. On both cut faces, in crisp pixel art: the endothelium with small dark nuclei, the white wavy internal elastic lamina, the banded red smooth-muscle media and the tan fibrous adventitia. Inside, the back half of the lumen is a smooth, softly lit pink-red lining with a faint plasma haze; glossy dark-red biconcave red cells tumble downward, surging on each beat. Text: *Normal artery*.

### S05 · 0:12–0:15 · CHRONIC HIGH GLUCOSE
**Camera:** hold, same view.
**Prompt:** Small yellow hexagonal glucose rings appear among the red cells, more with every beat; a small pixel hourglass beside the caption drains to mark years passing. The glucose stays dissolved in the blood — it never clumps or blocks anything. Text: *Chronic high glucose*.

### S06 · 0:15–0:18 · ENDOTHELIAL DYSFUNCTION
**Camera:** slow push to the wall.
**Prompt:** Close on the lining: soft orange patches spread over one segment of the endothelium; two round pale-lavender white blood cells with darker nuclei roll along the surface and stick there. Text: *Endothelial dysfunction*.

### S07 · 0:18–0:21 · PLAQUE FORMATION
**Camera:** pull back.
**Prompt:** Small yellow lipid particles slip from the blood under the damaged lining into the wall; a pale macrophage takes them up and turns into a speckled yellow foam cell inside the intima; on the cut face the inner wall begins to bulge toward the channel. Everything stays inside the wall. Text: *Plaque builds in the wall*.

### S08 · 0:21–0:24 · CHRONIC PROGRESSION
**Camera:** hold, the same angle as scene 4 (a direct before/after).
**Prompt:** On the cut face the plaque grows within the wall on one side — a cream fibrous cap over a yellow lipid core with cholesterol clefts and foam cells — and round opaque white calcium nodules appear in it; the channel narrows and shifts toward the opposite side; the lining still covers the plaque; an orange haze marks the diseased segment on the lumen surface. The hourglass turns again. Text: *Progression over time*.

### S09 · 0:24–0:27 · STENOSIS
**Camera:** hold; the normal artery slides in beside the diseased one.
**Prompt:** Two artery cutaways side by side at the same scale: left normal, right narrowed by the calcified plaque. In the narrowed one, fewer red cells pass, and short white streaks show the faster jet through the open but narrow channel. Text: *STENOSIS* (large, orange underline).

### S10 · 0:27–0:30 · OCCLUSION
**Camera:** pan down the same artery, then a slow push to the top of the block.
**Prompt:** Farther downstream, a segment is filled end to end with an orange-brown fibrous plug with wavy fibres and white calcium nodules; the channel tapers into it in a V and stops; the wall layers still outline the vessel's course on both sides. Above the block the red cells stack up and stand almost still, only rocking with each beat (no net flow). Text: *OCCLUSION* (large, orange underline).

### S11 · 0:30–0:33 · COLLATERAL BYPASS
**Camera:** pull back.
**Prompt:** Two pink side channels around the blocked segment — present all along — widen into curved bypasses from above the block to below it; a thin stream of red cells travels through them and on, slowly, below the block. The block itself stays closed. Text: *Collateral flow*.

### S12 · 0:33–0:36 · WALKING INCREASES DEMAND
**Camera:** cut to a side view tracking the walking legs.
**Prompt:** The patient walks again; his right leg is glass showing red-brown calf muscles that turn orange and pulse with each step and the red arteries inside; his left leg is solid. At the top, under the caption, two bars without numbers: an orange *Demand* bar (muscle icon) climbs with each step past a fixed white mark, a cyan *Supply* bar (drop icon) stays at that mark; the overflow of the demand bar pulses orange. Text: *Demand exceeds supply*, *Demand*, *Supply*.

### S13 · 0:36–0:39 · REST RELIEVES THE SYMPTOM
**Camera:** hold, close on the calves.
**Prompt:** He stands still and slowly straightens; his hand leaves the calf; the orange glow fades; the demand bar falls back below the unchanged supply mark. Nothing in the arteries changes. Text: *Rest reduces demand*.

### S14 · 0:39–0:42 · CT ANGIOGRAPHY
**Camera:** hold, a camera fixed in the room beside the gantry.
**Prompt:** A generic grey CT gantry (no brand) with its table; the patient lies on his back under a pale sheet, head first, and slides into the bore (his head is inside, never seen); his right forearm rests on an arm board with an IV cannula; a contrast injector on a stand beside the table is connected by tubing that fills white-blue as the injection starts. Text: *CT angiography*.

### S15 · 0:42–0:45 · CONTRAST CIRCULATION
**Camera:** slow lateral move and descent.
**Prompt:** A glass torso: see-through pink lungs, a steel-blue right heart and veins, a red left heart and aorta continuing down to the iliac arteries; a white-blue contrast front travels arm vein → superior vena cava → right heart → pulmonary arteries → lungs → pulmonary veins → left heart → aorta and down toward the legs. White-blue is a teaching colour.

### S16 · 0:45–0:48 · PELVIS-TO-FOOT FILLING
**Camera:** vertical tracking from the pelvis down to the feet.
**Prompt:** Inside a dark-navy glass body, the arterial tree from the aorta to both feet: a translucent red wall around each lumen; the lumen casts turn from blood red to white-blue as the contrast front moves down; yellow-orange plaque sleeves, white calcium nodules and the orange-brown occlusion plug sit in the walls; on the right leg the vessels below the popliteal block fill later, through the collaterals.

### S17 · 0:48–0:51 · CTA RECONSTRUCTION
**Camera:** pull back while the image turns slowly.
**Prompt:** A volume-rendered CTA reconstruction on near-black: both legs' arteries from the aorta to the feet in smooth ivory-to-white shading, faint dithered bones (pelvis, femurs, tibiae), calcium the brightest white with a dark outline; viewer corner brackets, a tick ruler, *R* on the viewer's left and *L* on the right. Text: *Arterial reconstruction*, *R*, *L*.

### S18 · 0:51–0:54 · STENOSIS ON CTA
**Camera:** fast push to the right superficial femoral artery, then a slow orbit.
**Prompt:** The ivory artery narrows to a thin but continuous channel with bright white calcium nodules in its wall; an orange slice line crosses it, and a dashed leader opens an axial greyscale slice: femur, muscle, the vein beside the artery, an eccentric small open lumen, grey plaque, a bright calcium arc. Text: *STENOSIS*.

### S19 · 0:54–0:57 · OCCLUSION ON CTA
**Camera:** move down the artery to behind the knee, then a slow orbit.
**Prompt:** The ivory contrast column of the popliteal artery stops; a faint cyan glass outline keeps the blocked segment's course; thinner collaterals curve around it and refill the artery below. Text: *OCCLUSION*.

### S20 · 0:57–1:00 · NORMAL VERSUS DIABETIC PAD
**Camera:** pull back to the final framing.
**Prompt:** Two right-leg arterial trees side by side at the same scale and angle, each inside a dark-navy glass leg, divided by a dotted line: left a normal teaching tree with smooth white-blue lumens in red walls; right this patient's tree with the mid-thigh stenosis, the popliteal occlusion with enlarged collaterals, and the below-knee narrowings and calcification. Text: *Normal*, *Diabetic PAD*.

### S21 · 1:00–1:03 · FINAL DIAGNOSTIC IMAGE
**Camera:** hold while both trees turn slightly together.
**Prompt:** In turn on the beat, the stenosis and then the occlusion light up orange with a bracket beside each, then a dotted orange route traces the collateral bypass; the title settles at the top; the music resolves. Ends on this image (`docs/final_still_labeled.png`, `docs/final_still_clean.png`). Text: *Diabetic Peripheral Arterial Disease*.
