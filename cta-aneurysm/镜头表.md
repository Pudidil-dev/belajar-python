# 镜头表 · Shot list — HOW DOES CT ANGIOGRAPHY DETECT A BRAIN ANEURYSM?

*From Electrons and Iodine Atoms to a Diagnostic Image.* Hook: *How can a scan reveal this tiny bulge?*

A silent 16-bit pixel-art explainer:
- **Format.** 1080×1920 vertical (9:16), 60 fps, exactly 180 s: 45 connected scenes of exactly 4 s.
- **Pixels.** Every frame is drawn at 360×640 and upscaled exactly 3× with nearest-neighbour. One limited palette, 4×4 ordered dithering, no anti-aliasing, no motion blur. Motion is stepped where the sprite language wants it; the camera stays on the pixel grid (integer translation, re-rasterized zooms, dithered dissolves).
- **Text.** No narration, no dialogue, no subtitles. Each scene has at most one short context label in **Azerbaijani** (1–4 words), plus a few part labels next to the thing they name. The labels are drawn by the editor layer (`CAP`), never baked into the pictures, and `?notext=1` renders the same film with no text at all.
- **Sound.** An original 120 BPM score and picture-synced sound effects, synthesized in code from the MRI film's instrument set (`mri-brain/tools/synth_audio.py`): kick, clap, reese and pulse bass, supersaw stabs, bell, glass and pluck. The hook is a 3-3-2 figure that the effects answer in the same key.
- **Music grid.** One beat is 0.5 s, one bar 2 s, one scene 2 bars: 180 s = 360 beats = 90 bars of 4/4. Every scene boundary is a bar line.
- **Loop.** Scene 45 lands on the exact opening composition: same artery network, same bulge, same camera, same colours. The last bar of music runs into the first.

## The one example that runs through the film

**One patient, computed.** The head is the procedural adult head of the CT film (`engine/lib/head.js`: skull, brain, ventricles, sinuses, scalp, in millimetres). It now has cerebral arteries (`engine/lib/ves.js`):
- both internal carotid arteries (cervical, petrous, cavernous, supraclinoid);
- middle cerebral arteries (M1 → bifurcation → superior and inferior M2 trunks → M3 branches);
- anterior cerebral arteries (A1, anterior communicating artery, A2, pericallosal);
- vertebral arteries → basilar artery → posterior cerebral arteries, with posterior communicating arteries closing the circle of Willis.

**One aneurysm.** One schematic, unruptured, saccular aneurysm at the **right middle cerebral artery bifurcation**:
- a narrow neck on the bifurcation and a rounder dome pointing laterally and down;
- it exists before the contrast arrives, it never grows, never ruptures, never moves;
- the same parent artery, the same two branches, the same neck and dome in every scene that shows it.

**Every image comes from that one model.** Each point has a CT number (HU): air −1000, CSF 8, white matter 26, grey matter 38, blood 40, cortical bone 1600. Arterial blood gets `+ 330 × enhancement` HU while the iodine bolus passes (an illustrative arterial-phase value, not a patient measurement). From the model come:
- the 3D vessel network of the opening and the ending;
- the projections, the sinogram and the filtered-backprojection reconstruction at the aneurysm's level;
- the axial, coronal and sagittal panels, the maximum-intensity projection and the vessel rendering of the review.

## Physics and medicine fixed for the whole film

- **Scanner.** A representative rotating-source multidetector CT, third-generation geometry: tube and curved detector arc face each other on one rotating frame inside a stationary ivory housing; the patient never moves while the frame turns.
- **Tube.**
  - **Cathode.** A heated tungsten filament releases electrons (thermionic emission); the heat frees them, it does not create them.
  - **Acceleration.** The tube voltage pulls them from the negative cathode to the positive anode across a vacuum. They get fast, never as fast as light.
  - **Anode.** In the rotating tungsten target an electron swings past a nucleus, is deflected and slows. The energy it loses leaves as an X-ray photon (bremsstrahlung, "braking radiation"). The electron does not turn into the photon, and it does not hit the nucleus.
  - **Heat.** Most of the electrons' energy becomes heat (about 99 %). Bremsstrahlung is the mechanism shown; characteristic X-rays also come from the target.
- **Beam.** A metal filter removes part of the low-energy photons; collimator blades intercept rays outside the selected field.
- **Contrast.**
  - **Route.** Iodine-containing contrast is injected into an arm vein. It travels arm vein → superior vena cava → right heart → pulmonary arteries → lung capillaries (inside the vessels, never the air spaces) → pulmonary veins → left heart → aorta → carotid and vertebral arteries → cerebral arteries.
  - **No catheter.** No catheter goes into the head.
  - **No seeking.** The contrast mixes with the plasma and fills whatever lumen the blood fills, the sac included. It does not seek out the aneurysm and does not create it.
  - **Not glowing.** It does not glow and is not radioactive: it is not a PET tracer and makes no radiation of its own.
  - **Violet is a code.** The violet is an educational colour code, not the colour of real contrast.
- **Timing.** The scan is timed to the arterial phase (high arterial enhancement). No universal delay, injection rate or dose is shown.
- **Interactions** (schematic atoms, not to scale; the scanner never images single atoms):
  - **Photoelectric absorption.** The photon is absorbed by an inner-shell electron of iodine (Z = 53, K edge 33.2 keV), which is ejected and leaves a vacancy. The photon ends there.
  - **Compton scattering.** In soft tissue a photon gives part of its energy to an outer electron (the recoil electron) and continues on a new path with lower energy. It is a different event from absorption.
  - **Attenuation.** Iodine attenuates the beam much more than blood or soft tissue (NIST mass attenuation data), so fewer primary photons cross an enhanced lumen. Some still pass.
- **Detector.** A conventional energy-integrating detector, not photon counting:
  - the scintillator absorbs the X-ray and emits visible light (inside the detector, never in the patient);
  - the silicon photodiode absorbs the light and makes electron–hole pairs that separate and are collected (a hole is an effective charge carrier, not a cavity);
  - the readout integrates the charge per view and digitizes it into one measurement.
- **Images.**
  - **Projections.** Each angle gives a different transmission profile; the profiles stacked by angle are projection data (a sinogram), not an image.
  - **Reconstruction.** A simplified filtered backprojection turns them into a cross-section of attenuation values. It is an explanatory picture, not a vendor's algorithm.
  - **Grayscale.** Grayscale is a display convention: blood is not white inside the patient.
  - **Volume and processed views.** Contiguous slices form a volume; MIP and vessel rendering are processed displays of it. Hiding bone is a display operation (the skull is still there), and the source images stay in view.
- **Interpretation.** A radiologist (seen only as a hand and a cursor) reviews the planes, traces the arteries and inspects and characterizes the outpouching (size, neck, branch relationships). No automatic "AI detected" banner, no numbers invented for the patient.
- **Proportion.**
  - **Radiation.** The X-rays are ionizing, so the exposure is justified and optimized. Zero risk is never claimed; harm is never dramatized.
  - **Contrast.** Contrast safety is assessed before the scan; the kidneys clear the contrast over time.
  - **Limits.** Small findings near bone and motion can be hard to see; further imaging depends on the question.
  - **Care.** CTA informs care and repairs nothing. Detection does not predict a rupture date and does not make treatment automatic.
- **Time.** Four-second scenes are not real interaction, scan, reconstruction or clearance times.
